ABSTRACT: To address the global energy crisis and valorize
industrial and agricultural solid waste, a corn straw-oil sludge
composite fuel (CF) with a mass ratio of 1.5:1 was prepared. Pure
corn straw fuel (SF) served as the control. The pyrolysis
characteristics of both pellet fuels up to 1000 °C were
systematically investigated via thermogravimetric (TG) analysis at
heating rates of 10, 20, and 30 °C/min. The pyrolysis characteristics were analyzed via characteristic parameters, Gaussian fitting
assignments, and the Flynn−Wall−Ozawa (FWO) and Kissinger−
Akahira−Sunose (KAS) model-free kinetic methods. Within the
tested temperature range (50−1000 °C), CF exhibited higher
characteristic parameters than SF, suggesting facile initiation under
the conditions tested. A positive deviation between the
experimental and calculated TG curves preliminarily indicated a synergistic effect during the core copyrolysis stage (170−650
°C). Gaussian fitting was tentatively assigned to four bond cleavage stages for both fuels, with the cleavage of Cal−heteroatom bonds
as the dominant reaction (proportion >50%). The addition of oil sludge introduced more alkyl-substituted aromatic compounds into
CF, increasing the proportion of Car−Cal and Car−heteroatom bond cleavage from 6.8% (SF) to 15.8% (CF). The average apparent
activation energy of CF was higher than that of SF. Notably, CF exhibited a kinetic characteristic of low initial activation energy and
high overall activation energy, which favored facile pyrolysis initiation and inhibition of side reactions.

1. INTRODUCTION
The intensifying global energy crisis has spurred significant
research interest in recovering energy from solid wastes.
Agricultural biomass, due to its abundance and carbon
neutrality, has garnered widespread attention.1 While biopressed fuel is a common treatment, its low calorific value limits
its high-value applications.2 Concurrently, oil sludge, a viscous,
hazardous waste generated during petroleum production,
storage, and transportation, contains substantial organic matter
(e.g., hydrocarbons). Incineration and solvent extraction are
conventional treatments, but high costs and environmental risks
constrain their efficiency and sustainability.3
Pyrolysis is a thermochemical process that decomposes
organic substances into valuable products under high-temperature and oxygen-free conditions.4,5 It has emerged as a
promising and feasible technology for the efficient treatment
of agricultural biomass and oil sludge.6−10 Copyrolysis, a process
involving two or more feedstocks, can induce synergistic effects
among the components.11,12 Copyrolysis of agricultural biomass
and oil sludge can complement each other: biomass provides
abundant volatiles, while oil sludge contributes a high calorific
value and a stable carbon structure, potentially improving
pyrolysis characteristics and product quality.13
Previous studies have explored various aspects of copyrolysis.
For instance, Rong et al.14 demonstrated that copyrolysis of rice

At present, relevant studies on the copyrolysis of agricultural
biomass and oil sludge have focused on the effects of blending
ratio, heating rate, and types of biomass on pyrolysis weight loss
and product distribution.22−27 Nevertheless, the majority of
previous copyrolysis experiments were performed using loose
powder mixtures. Powder samples differ substantially from
densified pellets in particle−particle contact status, internal
heat-mass transfer, and intercomponent reaction environment.
Conclusions from powder tests cannot completely represent the
practical thermal conversion behaviors of densified pellet fuels
used in industrial reactors. Our previous work28 focused on the
pelletization method and clarified an optimal preparation
method for a composite pellet fuel (CF) derived from corn
straw and oil sludge. Its thermochemical reactivity, component
interaction mechanism, and intrinsic kinetic features remain
unclear, which hinders the further engineering application of this
waste-derived pellet fuel.
This work emphasizes how oil sludge incorporation alters the
pyrolysis behavior of the pellet fuels. The pyrolysis characteristics of the composite pellet fuel (CF) derived from corn straw
and oil sludge are investigated and compared to those of
conventional corn straw pellet fuel (SF). The primary research
objectives are as follows: (1) to characterize the pyrolytic mass
loss profiles and characteristic parameters of SF and CF under
different heating rates; (2) to reveal potential interaction effects
between corn straw and oil sludge during copyrolysis; (3) to
analyze bond cleavage behaviors of the two fuels by means of
Gaussian fitting; and (4) to determine kinetic parameters using

the FWO and KAS methods and further assess their pyrolysis
reactivity and thermal stability. The outcomes of this work are
anticipated to offer theoretical foundations for the efficient
energy recovery of corn straw and oil sludge through copyrolysis
technology.

2. EXPERIMENTAL SECTION
2.1. Sample Preparation
Corn straw was collected from the vicinity of Jilin City, China, with its
main components measured as cellulose 29.66%, hemicellulose 34.71%,
and lignin 23.98% (NY/T 3494-2019). Oil sludge was a viscous
sediment collected from the bottom of a crude-oil storage tank at
PetroChina Jilin Petrochemical Company, with an oil content of 36.6%
(Soxhlet extraction method). Our previous work28 demonstrated that a
composite fuel with a calorific value equivalent to that of bituminous
coal can be fabricated by blending dried oil sludge with corn straw
powder, as shown in Figure 1. Corn straw was first rinsed with distilled
water to eliminate surface contaminants and then sieved to a particle
size of 150 mesh (0.106 mm), followed by moisture conditioning to a
water content of 10%. The corn straw/oil sludge mass ratio was fixed at
1.5:1 (60/40, w/w) based on our previous pelletization study.
Petroleum ether was introduced in a small amount to lower the
viscosity of oil sludge and subsequently eliminated. Corn straw and the
treated oil sludge were then batch blended and continuously stirred for
10 min to guarantee sufficient homogeneity of the mixture. The asprepared mixtures, designated composite fuel (CF), were compacted
into cylindrical pellets (6 mm in diameter and 20 mm in length) at
ambient temperature. The addition of oil sludge significantly enhanced
the physical and mechanical performance of the pellets, such as energy
density, mechanical durability, and ash fusion temperature. The results
showed that while higher oil sludge content (>40%) improved calorific
value, it led to excessive ash content (>12%) and poor pellet mechanical
durability, failing to meet the Chinese agricultural industry standard for
biomass pellet fuels (NY/T 2909-2016), which requires ash content

2.6. Model-Free Methods
Two model-free methods, the Flynn−Wall−Ozawa (FWO) and
Kissinger−Akahira−Sunose (KAS) methods, were used to analyze
the pyrolysis process and kinetic characteristics. Without assuming a
specific reaction mechanism, kinetic parameters can be calculated.
Nevertheless, it should be noted that these approaches have inherent
limitations. The derived apparent activation energy represents a lumped
parameter contributed by multiple overlapping parallel and sequential
reactions, rather than the intrinsic activation energy of a single
elementary reaction. Moreover, the isoconversional hypothesis assumes
constant activation energy at a fixed conversion, which may mask
pathway shifts within narrow conversion intervals, and cannot
independently obtain the reaction order or pre-exponential factor.

respectively.33,34 The linear relationships between the values of ln(β) or

3.2. Interaction between the Feedstocks

As shown in Figure 3a, the TG and DTG curves of experimental
raw material obtained at the heating rate of 20 °C/min were
enumerated and analyzed. It should be particularly noted that
the weight loss percentage of oil sludge (88.75%) was higher
than that of SF and CF. The evaporation process began at a
lower temperature (150 °C), which was due to the presence of
lighter oil components in the sludge. The primary weight loss
occurred between 200 and 550 °C. Compared with SF and CF,
oil sludge exhibited a much broader and less intense weight loss
peak, with a maximum weight loss rate of approximately 6 wt
%/min and a peak temperature shifted to 433.6 °C. This reveals
the complex organic composition and high heavy-fraction
content of oil sludge, which results in slow thermal
decomposition and low thermal conversion activity. Above
550 °C, the weight loss rate rapidly approached zero as the major
organic decomposition finished. The curve remained stable from
800 to 1000 °C, and only marginal weight loss near 1000 °C was
ascribed to the high-temperature decomposition of residual
inorganic minerals. The pyrolysis characteristic parameters for
experimental raw material are listed in Table 5.

4. CONCLUSIONS
In this study, the pyrolysis characteristics of SF and CF (corn
straw/oil sludge = 60/40, w/w) were systematically investigated
via TG experiments, combined with weight loss behaviors, their
Gaussian-fitting results, and FWO/KAS kinetic analysis. Their
pyrolysis characteristics were compared, with the main
conclusions as follows:
(1) SF and CF displayed outstanding weight loss behaviors in
the range of 170−650 °C, and their weight loss obtained
at different heating rates was all above 85%. Compared
with SF, the overall reaction processes of CF shifted
backward. Oil sludge improved the thermal stability and
overall pyrolysis performance of corn straw fuel. The
incorporation of oil sludge elevated the pyrolysis
characteristic parameters of SF.
(2) Synergistic effects (Δw > 0) existed between oil sludge
and corn straw with the proceeding copyrolysis process
(170−650 °C), with the experimental TG curve of CF
higher than the calculated one. The Δw (Δw = +3.35%)
exhibited a distinct maximum at approximately 460 °C,
corresponding to the peak decomposition of heavy
organics in oil sludge.
(3) The bond cleavage reactions of SF and CF involved the
release of free volatiles and cleavage of carboxyl, Cal−
heteroatom, S−S, Cal−Cal, Car−Cal, and Car−heteroatom
bonds, respectively. Thereinto, the decomposition of
aliphatic compounds was the most prominent behavior
during pyrolysis, and their proportions exceeded 50%. Oil
sludge introduced alkyl-substituted aromatic compounds
into CF, increasing the proportion of Car−Cal and Car−
heteroatom bond cleavage from 6.8% (SF) to 15.8%.
(4) CF exhibited a higher average activation energy than SF,
along with a distinctive kinetic profile: lower initial
activation energy (at α < 0.4) and higher overall activation
energy. This characteristic offers tangible advantages for
industrial-scale pyrolysis: (a) reduced preheating energy
consumption due to facile initiation; (b) suppressed
coking and side reactions due to higher midlate stage
energy barriers. These findings position the corn straw-oil
sludge composite fuel as a promising candidate for wasteto-energy applications, though future work should address
scale-up challenges, including heat/mass transfer limitations and continuous reactor performance.

initial increase and subsequent decrease over α = 0.1−0.7,

pyrolysis (α < 0.5): decomposition of light components with low
energy barriers; (b) midterm pyrolysis (α = 0.5−0.7):
decomposition of cellulose, lignin, and aromatic compounds,
plus secondary cracking of tar (high energy barriers); and (c)
final pyrolysis (α > 0.8): inorganic mineral decomposition
became the dominant factor leading to a rise in activation
energy. At α < 0.4, the activation energy of CF was lower than
that of SF, meaning CF required less energy to initiate pyrolysis.
At α ≥ 0.4, the activation energy of CF was higher than that of
SF, meaning CF had a higher energy barrier for subsequent
reactions. The addition of oil sludge brought more light and
heavy components compared with cellulose, hemicellulose, and
lignin. The higher proportions of weak, Car−Cal, and Car−
heteroatom bonds of CF in Table 6 verified this conclusion.
For CF, the apparent activation energy generally increased at
the early stage of conversion, reached a local maximum around α
= 0.5, and then decreased before increasing sharply at high
conversion. Combined with the phenomenological DTG
deconvolution, the relatively low energy barrier at low
conversion is consistent with the decomposition of weakly
bound and more reactive components, whereas the higher
activation energy at intermediate conversion may reflect the
increasing contribution of more thermally resistant aliphatic and
aromatic structures. This low initial and high overall activation
energy of CF is a highly favorable kinetic feature for practical
application: (a) Facile pyrolysis initiation: Low initial activation
energy reduces the preheating energy consumption and avoids
harsh initial reaction conditions. (b) Inhibition of side reactions:
High midlate activation energy suppresses the nonselective
cracking of organic components and the formation of coking
precursors. These findings position the corn straw-oil sludge
composite fuel as a promising candidate for waste-to-energy
applications, though future work should address scale-up
challenges, including heat/mass transfer limitations and
continuous reactor performance.
