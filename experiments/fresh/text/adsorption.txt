Cite This: ACS Omega 2026, 11, 65055−65067

ABSTRACT: Organic matter dissolution by oxidants can enhance
the coal permeability. However, as coalbed methane (CBM) is
predominantly reserved in the adsorbed state, the organic matter
dissolution changes the adsorption−desorption equilibrium, thus
affecting the CBM production. Current studies have not sufficiently
addressed the combined variations in permeability and adsorption
capacity after oxidation, which are critical for enhancing CBM
production via oxidation. Experiments on coal permeability,
molecular structures, adsorption capacity, wettability, and pores
were conducted. The results demonstrated that new pores and
fractures were formed after oxidation, as evidenced by an average
increase in porosity of 15.57%, leading to an average increase of
161.16% in the initial permeability (k0, at atmospheric pressure).
However, the increased pore size and oxygenated functional group content lead to a decreasing contact angle (CA) and specific
surface area (SSA), ultimately causing an average reduction in adsorption capacity of 72.20%. Changes in k0/CA were negatively
correlated with moisture content (Mad) + ash yield (Ad) and positively correlated with fat/oxygenated functional group (FA/OFG).
The decreasing CA indicates significantly enhanced hydrophilicity, inhibiting depressurization by drainage, and negatively affecting
CBM production. The change in k0/the change in CA after oxidation generally exceeds 10 when Mad + Ad < 14% and FA/OFG >
0.5, which is favorable for CBM production enhancement under similar treatment conditions in this study. These findings determine
the coals to be more suitable for oxidation with equal treatment and offer insights for assessing the CBM production enhancement
potential via oxidation.

1.. INTRODUCTION
Unlike conventional natural gas reservoirs (such as sandstone
and carbonates), the generally low permeability of coal inhibits
the efficient production of coalbed methane (CBM).1−4 CBM
has been exploited commercially in the USA, Canada, and
Australia, optimizing the fossil energy consumption structures.5−7 CBM resources in China are abundant, but affected
by multiple tectonic movements and high stresses, the in situ
coal permeability is mostly low, restricting gas migration,
resulting in low CBM production in many areas.8−10
Therefore, enhancing the in situ permeability of coal is critical
for efficient CBM exploitation.
Compared with the method primarily utilized in CBM
exploitation (hydraulic fracturing), chemical treatment (acidization and oxidation) can enhance the permeability of coal on
a large scale. Coal is mainly composed of organic matter and
minerals, and minerals (especially carbonate and silicate
minerals) dissolved by acid to form new pores and fractures
can enhance the permeability. Oxidation treatment mainly
leads to organic matter dissolution, accompanied by the

content in coal is mostly low, and the increase in permeability

current studies are mostly H2O2, NaClO, KMnO4, and
Na2S2O8, and differences in reservoir composition led to

Some organic matter is dissolved by the oxidants, which is
determined by the increasing concentration of dissolved
organic matter in the solution, leading to pore and fracture
formation and significantly increasing permeability in most
samples after the oxidation treatment.13,16,19 For example, the
porosity of coal from the Surat Basin, Australia, increased from

permeability of coal from the Tarim Basin, NW China, was

effects of reservoir parameter permeability changes after the
oxidation treatment is essential.
CBM is mainly absorbed in the nanopores developed in
organic matter, and the organic matter dissolution caused by
oxidants alters the adsorption−desorption equilibrium, further
affecting the CBM production. However, the synergistic
variations in the coal permeability and adsorption capacity
after oxidation treatment are rarely reported. Since CBM is
generally desorbed by drainage and depressurizing to convert
to free gas and transport to the CBM well,22−25 the adsorption
capacity is important for CBM resource evaluation. The pore
sizes increased after the oxidation treatment, reducing the
adsorption capacity. However, the changes in molecular
structure caused by the oxidation treatment generally change
the wettability and then affect the adsorption capacity.26−30
Many studies have proven that oxidation treatment is an
effective approach for increasing permeability, but the
combined variations in permeability and adsorption capacity
need to be clarified, which is critical for determining the
reservoirs suitable for the oxidation treatment.
In this study, eight samples were collected from different
coal mines in China, and the physical and chemical properties
of the samples before and after oxidation treatment were
compared. Permeability variations were analyzed, and changes
in adsorption capacity and wettability were clarified on the
basis of isothermal adsorption and contact angle (CA) results.
Changes in the pore and molecular structures were determined
based on the low-field nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR),
low-temperature liquid nitrogen adsorption (LN2-adsorption),
and Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR) experiments. Subsequently, the mechanism of synergistic variations
in permeability and adsorption induced by organic matter
oxidation was clarified, and the effects of critical reservoir
parameters affecting permeability and wettability variations
were discussed. The results provide insights into assessing the
CBM production enhancement by the oxidation treatment.

4.2. Correlation between Variations in
Permeability/Wettability and Coal Properties

The varying changes in k0 indicated that reservoir parameters
had an essential impact on the oxidation treatment. The
reduction in SSA after the oxidation treatment promoted more
gas desorption at the same pressure, accelerating the CBM
production. However, the decreasing CA was not conducive to
water production from the reservoir, which is detrimental to
the CBM production. Therefore, the relationship between the
changes in k0/CA and the reservoir parameters was clarified.
