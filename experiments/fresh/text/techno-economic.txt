ABSTRACT: Climate change and greenhouse gas emissions are
critical global challenges, driving the need for innovative solutions
to reduce carbon footprints while meeting energy demands. This
study addresses these challenges by optimizing the CO2−WAG
(water-alternating gas) injection process, a technique that enhances
oil recovery while sequestering carbon dioxide in oil reservoirs. The
optimization framework integrates machine learning methods with
the non-dominated sorting genetic algorithm II (NSGA-II) to
simultaneously maximize cumulative oil production and CO2
sequestration, minimize water production, and ensure economic
viability through the net present value (NPV) objective function.
The study employs stacking learning to develop smart proxy
models, which significantly reduce computational time while maintaining high accuracy in predicting objective functions. These
models are trained on a comprehensive data set generated from reservoir simulations, enabling efficient optimization across diverse
reservoir conditions. The NSGA-II algorithm is used to generate a three-dimensional Pareto front, representing optimal trade-offs
between the conflicting objectives. To facilitate decision-making, a clustering-based approach is introduced, categorizing solutions
into groups such as gold, silver, and bronze based on their performance metrics. The results demonstrate the effectiveness of the
proposed framework, with the NSGA-II algorithm producing 500 optimal solutions on the Pareto front. Among these, 60 solutions
are identified as gold, offering the best balance between technical and economic objectives. Compared to previous studies, the
introduced framework significantly improves computational efficiency and prediction accuracy, reducing optimization time while
maintaining high precision in the results. This approach not only enhances the accuracy of CO2−WAG optimization but also
provides a scalable and adaptable framework for sustainable oil recovery and carbon management in various reservoir settings.

1. INTRODUCTION
Global warming, driven by rising greenhouse gas concentrations, particularly carbon dioxide (CO2), poses a severe
threat to the climate system. Since the Industrial Revolution,
fossil fuel combustion has significantly increased atmospheric
CO2 levels, leading to rising temperatures, melting ice caps,
and extreme weather events. Despite international efforts like
the Paris Agreement (2015) and initiatives by organizations
such as the International Energy Agency (IEA),1 the excessive
accumulation of greenhouse gases, particularly CO2, as a
primary driver of climate change and global warming.2−4
Among the types of greenhouse gases, carbon dioxide has the
most significant effect on increasing Earth’s temperature.5,6
Concerns about climate change and the increase in Earth’s
temperature have forced humanity to confront and prevent
these challenges. One of the first steps toward solving this
problem is to use clean or renewable energy sources and
reduce the use of fossil fuels. However, due to the increasing
need for humans to consume energy and the high cost of clean

which are typical sources of hydrocarbon production, saline
aquifers, and coal beds that cannot be extracted.10−12 CO2
sequestration has two general injection processes: 1 carbon
dioxide injection to enhance hydrocarbon recovery and 2
carbon dioxide injection only for carbon sequestration.
Although fossil fuels are gradually losing importance in the
energy transition, they will remain part of the global energy
mix, even in the best scenarios projected by the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC).13 Therefore,
well-designed CO2-EOR projects can offer dual benefits:
maximizing oil production and enhancing CO2 sequestration.
Key questions include how to achieve an optimal design and
what features such a design should have. To address these,
decision parameters and operational controls must prioritize
maximizing both oil recovery and CO2 storage while balancing
technical and economic considerations.
Past studies on optimizing CO2−WAG-EOR processes fall
into two categories: earlier studies using statistical methods
and more recent studies employing meta-heuristic algorithms
and artificial intelligence (AI). Bedrikovetsky et al. (1996)
developed a mathematical model for WAG injection, focusing
on oil recovery and the gas-oil mobility ratio. They found that
increasing the water-to-gas injection ratio not only reduces oil
recovery but also improves gas phase control, highlighting the
need for an optimal ratio. Similarly, gas slug size inversely
affected the objective functions, emphasizing the complexity of
balancing operational parameters.14 Kovscek and Wang (2005)
studied to determine the best CO2 injection scenarios that lead
to maximum oil recovery and maximum CO2 storage in the
reservoir.15 Homian et al. (2008) used response surface
analysis to study the impact of operational parameters like the
water−gas injection ratio, gas slug volume, hysteresis, and
reservoir heterogeneity on WAG injection optimization at the
field scale.16 Ghaderi et al. (2012) evaluated CO2-EOR
performance in a low-permeability reservoir, focusing on oil
recovery, CO2 storage, and net present value (NPV) as key
objective functions.17 Dai et al. (2014) developed an integrated
statistical framework combining reservoir simulation, geological modeling, and response surface analysis to optimize
CO2-EOR in a depleted Texas oil reservoir. Their framework
analyzed the effects of operational and reservoir parameters on
oil recovery and CO2 sequestration separately.18
The framework of research in the past decade has changed.
Meta-heuristic optimization algorithms and artificial intelligence play a decisive role in the optimization of the CO2-EOR
process. Extensive studies have investigated the role of these
tools and measured their capabilities.19−22 Carbon sequestration has emerged as a key objective function in CO2-EOR
projects alongside oil production and is now a primary
optimization criterion in many studies. Research on carbon
sequestration has expanded significantly, not only in CO2-EOR
but also in related areas like carbon storage in aquifers and
natural gas and hydrogen storage projects.23 The study by
Safarzadeh et al. (2014)24 did not employ an AI-based
approach but focused on CO2-EOR. It utilized stochastic
optimization algorithms and emphasized multiobjective
optimization within the CO2-EOR context. W. Ampomah et
al. (2017)20 incorporated an AI-based approach and focused
on CO2-EOR, concentrating on multiobjective optimization
and using neural networks to develop a smart proxy model.
Junyu You et al. (2020)19 adopted an AI-based approach and
worked on CO2-EOR, introducing an optimization framework

Optimizing a process involves finding the optimal values of
the control parameters to accomplish a specific goal.
Optimization aims to find the maximum or minimum value
of one or more objective functions of a specific process with
several control variables while respecting the operational
constraints of the process. The number of objective functions
is influential in determining the type of optimization process,
so the world of single-objective and multiobjective optimization is entirely distinct and separate. When we optimize a
problem for one objective, the solution is achieved by setting
the system control variables as the maximum and minimum

values of the objective function. Hence, a single-objective
optimization will give us a unique solution, while a multiobjective process will give us all the same mathematical
answers. Based on the theory of the Pareto front, these sets of
identical solutions will be placed on one front, and none of the
other solutions will overcome them. The WAG injection
process is a complex procedure with several control variables
for which several different objective functions can be
considered that have conflicting interests, so multiobjective
optimization is required in such a problem. Considering such
conditions for a CO2-EOR project, the decision variables
should be designed regarding the operational and economic
conditions to achieve the maximum value of the primary
objective functions, i.e., oil production and carbon dioxide
sequestration. One of the objective functions that have
received less attention in past studies is the cumulative water
production function. This objective function must be
minimized in a successful CO2−WAG-EOR operation.
Excessive production of water increases both operating costs
and damages the ecosystem due to high salinity. Therefore, this
study’s main issue is optimizing a CO2−WAG-EOR project to
achieve an ideal design to maximize carbon dioxide
sequestration and oil production and minimize water
production in the most economical possible conditions.
Achieving the mentioned objective functions considering the
economic conditions will not be possible without improving
the framework created in the previous study. This improvement includes enhancing the sensitivity analysis of decision
variables based on objective functions, constructing highprecision proxy models using the stacking learning method,

2. METHODOLOGY
The crucial step to start the process of multiobjective
optimization of CO2−WAG injection is the selection of
objective functions; in this study, three essential objective
functions were selected:
1 Cumulative amount of oil production (reach the
maximum)
2 The net amount of carbon dioxide sequestration. The
difference between injected and produced carbon
dioxide gas is considered the basis for calculating carbon
sequestration (reach the maximum)
3 Production of water with oil (to be minimized)
The three objective functions considered in this study are all
responsible for the technical supervision of the quality of the
CO2−WAG-EOR project’s quality. In order to economically
control the optimal solutions, a fourth objective function will
be calculated separately from the technical objective functions.
This objective function is the net present value (NPV), and the
purpose of calculating this function will be to create an
economic decision criterion on the solutions of the Pareto
front.
The optimization steps of the CO2−WAG-EOR process
using machine learning methods and multiobjective optimization algorithms include several main steps. The main steps of
optimization include the following:
1 Building a reservoir model to simulate CO2−WAG-EOR
operation
2 Sensitivity analysis and selection of decision parameters
and objective functions
3 Experiment design and data bank generation

operation boundaries or limits for the optimization of decision

2 The minimum limit for the parameters of the maximum

minimum miscible pressure (MMP) of the CO2 and
reservoir oil so that the operation does not go out of the
mixing state. Its maximum limit is determined based on
the conditions of pressure, reservoir production, and the
minimum economic rate of production from the field.

this study, a supervised machine learning approach is used to
evaluate decision variables and objective functions. Neighborhood component analysis (NCA) is a supervised learning
method for multivariate data classification into separate classes
based on a predefined distance metric from the data.
Functionally, it shares goals similar to those of the K-nearest
neighbors algorithm and directly utilizes a related concept
called random neighbors. NCA is a nonparametric method for
feature selection aimed at maximizing the prediction accuracy
of regression and classification algorithms. NCA, with the given
decision variables and objective functions, learns the relationship between them and provides a specific weight for each
decision variable’s effect on each objective function.

2.4. Smart Proxy Modeling. The development of proxy
models in the oil and gas industry, as well as in many other
industries that utilize numerical simulations, has a long
history.36,37 Proxy models provide fast approximate solutions,
which can replace large numerical simulation models. They
serve specific valuable purposes such as history matching,
uncertainty analysis, quantification, and optimization of
processes.38,39 By employing machine learning methods, a
precise, intelligent proxy model is constructed to accelerate the
optimization process. Machine learning methods are broadly
categorized into three main types: supervised learning,
unsupervised learning, and reinforcement learning. The field
of reservoir engineering utilizes supervised learning methods
for building smart proxy models. These methods are datadriven and are used for both classification and regression tasks.
In this study, a combination of neural networks and
ensemble learning methods is employed. We introduce these
two methods and describe how they are utilized in
constructing the intelligent proxy model. Among machine
learning methods, only neural networks can simultaneously
examine and perform regression on multiple outputs and
objective functions. In past studies, various neural network
architectures have been considered for optimizing CO2−
WAG-EOR operations. The architecture of a neural network
plays a crucial role in the accuracy of network predictions,
which can be controlled based on parameter adjustments.

objective functions within the framework of the study’s
computational calculations. The base models consist of three
neural networks with different activation functions, each
created in parallel with each other following the process
mentioned for building intelligent proxy models. In training
these networks, the training data are the same, and half of the
database is used.42 The other half of the data are used in
building the meta-model. Therefore, the three base models and
the meta-model form a set that will play the role of smart proxy
models. Additionally, the second proxy model will be used in
the single-objective optimization of the NPV.
2.5. Combining the Meta-Model and the Metaheuristic Optimization Algorithm. Optimization problems have
been studied using various approaches. The use of derivativebased methods for finding extreme points of the objective
function or various metaheuristic algorithms has effectively
optimized single-objective problems. However, there are
optimization problems that cannot be summarized in a
single-objective function. As mentioned in the introduction,
the presence of conflict of interest among the objective
functions has led to the consideration of multiobjective
optimization as the primary goal of the framework. However,
a single-objective optimization step will be involved in
optimizing the NPV function, which plays the role of the
trade-off parameter. The optimization tool in this study is one
of the powerful and well-known multiobjective optimization
algorithms, namely the non-dominated sorting genetic
algorithm version II (NSGA-II).43,44 Previously, Menad et. al
have successfully utilized the NSGA-II algorithm within the
optimization framework for CO2−WAG-EOR operations.21

as follows:30

Conduct a selection process utilizing binary comparisons.

Assemble the new population and perform selection

For the single-objective optimization step, the NPV function
will also be optimized using a single-objective genetic
optimization algorithm. By adjusting the parameters of the
single-objective and multiobjective optimization algorithms,
the generated meta-models must be combined as objective
functions with the optimization algorithm. The output of this
step for single-objective optimization in the NPV function will
be the maximum NPV value within the study’s range. In
multiobjective optimization, the Pareto front will represent a
set of desirable values for the three objective functions.
2.6. Establishing a Decision Criterion on the Pareto
Front. Using multiobjective optimization methods ultimately
results in a Pareto front, which consists of solutions that are
mathematically nondominated by other solutions. Given the
three objectives of this study, the primary focus is on
multiobjective optimization. Solutions on the Pareto front
are obtained through several stages, and changes in the
population size and optimization algorithm parameters lead to
changes in the Pareto front’s shape and optimal solutions. In
this process, solutions are evaluated based on multiple criteria,
and they are categorized into different clusters as follows:
1 Solutions with NPV higher than the single-objective
optimization’s maximum NPV are gold solutions.
2 Solutions with an NPV lower than the maximum NPV of
single-objective optimization but have cumulative oil
production exceeding ten percent of the reservoir
volume or carbon dioxide sequestration of more than
80% of the upper limit of the carbon injection range are
recognized as silver solutions.
3 Solutions with NPV below the average NPV in the
database are called black solutions.
4 Solutions that do not meet the conditions above and
have lower water production than the average reservoir
water production are categorized as bronze solutions.
5 If the reservoir water production does not satisfy the
fourth condition, it is a blue solution.
After the creation of a database of solutions, a decision tree
clustering algorithm is utilized to create a classification model
for Pareto front data. The decision tree algorithm, through the
database analysis, will generate new criteria different from the
initial criteria.

4. CONCLUSION
This study aimed to optimize the multiobjective process of
enhanced oil recovery with cyclic carbon dioxide injection and
water using machine learning methods and the multiobjective
optimization algorithm NSGA-II. The optimization objectives
were divided into technical and economic parts, including
cumulative oil production, cumulative water production, and
carbon dioxide sequestration as technical objectives, and the
net present value (NPV) as the economic objective. The main
results of the study are as follows:
1 The NCA algorithm results in the sensitivity analysis
phase showed that this method can identify the impact
of decision variables on individual objectives. The NCA
results predicted a strong correlation between NPV and
cumulative oil production before the multiobjective
optimization process, which was confirmed after the
optimization process.
2 Multilayer neural networks were used to create smart
proxy models for predicting the NPV objective function
for both reservoir models. Both models accurately
predicted this objective function, with regression values
more than 0.93.
3 Meta-models were constructed based on ensemble
learning to improve the prediction accuracy of the
cumulative water production objective and provide
similar accuracy for all three technical objectives. The
meta-models predicted the cumulative oil production
objective with a regression of 0.96, the carbon dioxide
sequestration objective with a regression of 0.99, and the
cumulative water production objective with an accuracy
of 0.95. The stacking learning-based meta-model
significantly improves prediction accuracy in complex
oil reservoirs, allowing for precise proxy models with
fewer data, thereby enhancing reservoir management
decisions.
4 The multiobjective optimization algorithm NSGA-II
provides valid Pareto fronts for the reservoir. Multiple
random experiments did not dominate these fronts.
Pareto front solutions could provide objective function
values more effectively than single-objective optimization results.
5 Previous studies have indicated a conflict between
cumulative oil production and carbon dioxide sequestration objective functions. The conflicting interests
between these objective functions were a solid reason

decision tree clustering model. This clustering model was
created and, through data analysis from the database, classifies

common point among all gold solutions is that, from the
decision tree’s perspective, the cumulative oil production
exceeds 376.283. This result also demonstrates that the NPV

Silver solutions, on the other hand, are influenced by the
carbon sequestration objective function. Therefore, by
modification of the NPV model and increasing the impact of
the carbon sequestration objective function, silver solutions
can be more prominent in selecting the optimal solution.
The decision tree clustering model was applied to the final
Pareto front. The results of the clustering model on the final
Pareto front are shown in Figure 16. With the current NPV

toward the cumulative oil production objective function

Figure 17, which displays the Pareto front on the coordinate
plane of cumulative oil production and carbon dioxide
sequestration, illustrates that some bronze and black solutions
have higher carbon sequestration than silver and gold
solutions. However, according to the economic model
considered, they are not in the gold group. The strong
inclination of gold solutions toward maximum values of
cumulative oil production is also evident in Figure 17.
The optimization framework was completed with the
clustering of the Pareto front solutions, determining 60 gold
solutions for the CO2−WAG -EOR operation and achieving
better performance in the multiobjective optimization
approach compared to single-objective optimization, demonstrating the effectiveness of the optimization method in the
study.

quantification of trade-offs between conflicting technical,
economic, and environmental objectives in CO2−WAG-EOR
operations. By leveraging the Pareto front and a decision tree
clustering model, the framework explicitly exposes the inherent
compromises between maximizing NPV (via oil production),
