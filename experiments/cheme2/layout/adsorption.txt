                                           Carbon nanotube (CNT) ﬁlms with interconnected three-dimensional (3D) networks are ideal porous
                                           scaﬀolds for high-entropy alloy (HEA) immobilization, enabling integrated adsorption and detoxiﬁcation
                                           of chemical warfare agents. Herein, FeCoNiCrMn HEA nanoparticles (50–150 nm) were uniformly
                                           anchored on 3D CNT ﬁlm networks via ultrafast carbothermal shock (CTs) within 55 ms. The obtained
                                           FeCoNiCrMn@CNTs show a single-phase face-centered cubic structure with near-equiatomic
                                           composition and multivalent metal sites. At 25 °C and 50% RH, the composite delivers a high equilibrium
                                           adsorption capacity of 636 mg g−1 for 2-chloroethyl ethyl sulﬁde (2-CEES) vapor, a sulfur mustard
                                           simulant. A 98.5% degradation eﬃciency is reached within 12 h via a tandem redox pathway: reductive
