ABSTRACT: Continuous Stirred Tank Reactors (CSTRs) are
widely used in chemical process industries, where precise
temperature control is crucial for product quality, operational
effectiveness, and safety. However, the accuracy of strict firstprinciple models is limited by the extremely nonlinear thermal
behavior brought on by Arrhenius-based kinetics, measurement
noise, transport delay, and feed fluctuations. In order to overcome
this, the current study uses multivariate pilot-plant time-series data
to create a high-fidelity nonlinear autoregressive model with
exogenous inputs (NARX). Twin Delayed Deep Deterministic
Policy Gradient (TD3) and Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) are used in the
NARX model as a reinforcement learning environment for closedloop temperature regulation. Feed temperatures, feed flow rates,
and hot-oil-bath actuation are among the various manipulated-variable combinations that are assessed. The suggested NARX−RL
framework effectively tracks set points, according to the results. Both SAC and TD3 exhibit acceptable closed-loop performance,
according to a comparative analysis; however, TD3 consistently outperforms SAC in tracking indices, convergence speed, and
control stability across the majority of manipulated-variable combinations. Overall, the proposed NARX-based reinforcement
learning technique provides a feasible option for flexible, data-driven, and practically implementable temperature regulation in labscale CSTR systems.

1.3. Data-Driven and Hybrid Modeling Approaches for
CSTR Control

Reference5 presents an implicit hybrid machine learning
technique for Continuous Stirred Tank Reactor (CSTR)
modeling that integrates physics-based conservation laws with
neural networks using an implicit trapezoidal formulation
solved through automatic differentiation and Newton
iterations. The proposed model captures stiff reactor dynamics
more effectively than an explicit RK4-based hybrid model,
producing lower training and testing prediction losses while
maintaining reliable performance in the presence of noisy data.
Moreover, ref5 demonstrates that the hybrid model can serve
as a surrogate environment for Proximal Policy Optimization
(PPO)-based reinforcement learning, where the learned policy
performs nearly as well as plant-trained RL and surpasses realtime optimization. Thus, ref5 highlights the effective
integration of hybrid modeling and reinforcement learning
for data-efficient nonlinear reactor optimization. Reference15
compares one fully data-driven model and three hybrid models
for catalytic CO2 methanation in a CSTR, with emphasis on
prediction accuracy, training effort, and reliability. The findings
in ref15 show that hybrid models incorporating mechanistic
knowledge do not always outperform purely data-driven
models, indicating that model selection should depend on
the available data and the specific process-modeling problem.
Reference25 proposes a black-box nonlinear CSTR identification method based on fractional neural networks and a
newly developed fractional activation function for improved
real-time modeling under challenging operating conditions.
The RLF tanh-based multilayer perceptron model in ref25
achieves improved prediction accuracy, generalization, noise
tolerance, reduced-data robustness, and faster training
compared with conventional tanh and existing fractionalactivation-based models, although automatic fractional-order
tuning and broader closed-loop validation remain necessary.

2. CSTR LAB-SCALE PILOT PLANT
The CSTR considered in this work is a lab scale Double
jacketed type shown in Figure 1.
The schematic diagram of the pilot plant CSTR is shown in
Figure 2.
The reactor vessel is borosilicate glass construction. This
resists corrosion, which means that the vessel can handle all
sorts of chemical reactions without breaking down over time.
Another advantage is that the glass provides complete visibility
of the process. This allows the operator to watch the reaction
happening inside in real time. They can observe changes in
color or any bubbles that may form. The progress of the
reaction is visualized by these indicators.
The control arrangement employs a double-jacketed reactor
architecture to ensure exact temperature regulation throughout
operation. While the outer jacket circulates a coolant or heattransfer fluid, such as water, oil, or another thermal medium,
the inner jacket holds the reaction mixture. The system can
heat or cool the reaction mixture as needed by varying the
temperature of this circulating fluid. In both exothermic and
endothermic processes, this promotes appropriate product
production and preserves the intended reaction kinetics.
Additionally, the outer jacket acts as an insulating barrier,
increasing energy efficiency and lowering heat loss to the
environment. Additionally, by avoiding direct exposure to
extremely high or low wall temperatures, it enhances operator
safety. Overall, the double-jacketed design maintains a stable,
predictable, and well-controlled thermal environment within
the reactor.

7. CONCLUSION AND FUTURE SCOPE
This study addressed a significant gap in the Continuous
Stirred Tank Reactor (CSTR) control literature by focusing on
intelligent temperature regulation in a highly nonlinear and
exothermic reactor environment. Although earlier studies have
independently demonstrated the effectiveness of model-based
control, data-driven modeling, and reinforcement learning,
their combined application to a practical lab-scale CSTR
remains comparatively limited. Many existing approaches
depend heavily on accurate first-principles models, simulation-only validation, or restricted operating conditions, which
limits their adaptability and real-time applicability. This
motivated the development of a more flexible, data-supported,
and control-oriented reinforcement learning framework for
CSTR temperature regulation.
To bridge this gap, a progressive modeling and control
strategy was adopted. First-principles modeling was initially
used to describe the fundamental mass and energy balance
dynamics of the reactor and to establish the thermal behavior
of the process. This was followed by gray-box and nonlinear
data-driven modeling approaches, culminating in a high-fidelity
NARX-based model capable of predicting the reactor temperature response under different manipulated-variable conditions. The NARX model served as a practical environment for
reinforcement learning controller training, thereby reducing
the direct dependence on a purely mechanistic model while
retaining the nonlinear input−output behavior of the
experimental CSTR system.

and Soft Actor-Critic (SAC), two sophisticated deep reinforcement learning algorithms, were studied for continuous
manipulated-variable control. To assess the closed-loop

and hot oil bath input, were taken into consideration. The
findings shown that SAC and TD3 could both effectively
control the reactor temperature for the stepwise set point
trajectory. While TD3 offered more deterministic control
behavior with consistent convergence features, SAC showed
considerable exploratory activity and adaptive control action.
From the comparative analysis, it was observed that the
TD3-based controller produced slightly superior tracking
indices for most of the manipulated-variable combinations
considered. In several cases, TD3 achieved lower values of
MAE, RMSE, ISE, IAE, and training time, indicating improved
tracking accuracy and faster convergence. SAC also provided
acceptable closed-loop performance, but its manipulatedvariable profiles were generally more oscillatory because of
the stochastic nature of the policy and entropy-driven
exploration. Therefore, while both reinforcement learning
controllers were effective, the overall trend suggests that TD3
offered a more balanced compromise between tracking
accuracy, convergence speed, and control stability for the
present CSTR temperature regulation problem.
Overall, the findings show that combining control-oriented
nonlinear modeling with reinforcement learning is a potential
approach to practical CSTR temperature management. The
suggested methodology lowers reliance on solely mechanistic
models, increases adaptability to disturbances and uncertainty,
and facilitates the move from simulation-oriented studies to
real-time deployable control structures. As a result, this study
reinforces the argument for reinforcement learning-based
temperature management of lab-scale Continuous Stirred
Tank Reactors as a timely and relevant direction in advanced
process control research.
Building on the current work, future studies can concentrate
on expanding the suggested reinforcement learning framework
to more comprehensive, adaptable, and practically applicable
reactor control scenarios. With a focus on sample efficiency,
convergence speed, robustness under severe disturbances, and
final control performance, additional comparisons between

TD3 and SAC and other sophisticated deep reinforcement
learning algorithms, such as Proximal Policy Optimization
(PPO), Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (DDPG), and
constrained reinforcement learning techniques, may be
conducted. A multivariable control framework that simultaneously regulates reactor temperature and reactant concentration using a variety of manipulated inputs, including feed
flow rates, feed temperatures, coolant flow rates, and heating/
cooling utility signals, can be developed from the current
single-variable temperature regulation strategy.
Moving from simulation to real and long-term implementation opens up further possibilities. Hardware-in-the-loop
implementation on industrial platforms such as PLCs or
edge devices can be used to assess real-time inference, latency,
and compatibility with current control infrastructure. The
framework can also be extended to include batch and
semibatch reactors, online transfer learning for adjusting to
catalyst deactivation, fouling, and sensor drift, decentralized
control of interconnected reactor networks, and the incorporation of physics-informed neural networks to improve
interpretability and reduce data requirements. Future research
should focus on safety-critical RL with formal constraint
handling and fail-safe mechanisms, as well as promoting longterm industrial viability through energy-aware rewards, model
compression, transfer learning, predictive maintenance, and
low-power edge deployment Table 15 .
