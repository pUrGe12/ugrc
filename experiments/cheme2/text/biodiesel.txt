A ternary GO-Gd2TiO5/g-C3N4 nanocomposite was developed as a recyclable heterogeneous nanocatalyst
for sustainable biodiesel production from waste cooking oil (WCO). The catalyst was comprehensively
characterized by structural, morphological, surface, thermal, and electrochemical techniques.
Electrochemical analysis using CV, GCD, and EIS demonstrated favorable interfacial behavior, with 97%
capacitance retention after 7000 cycles, a 0–100 mM linear response, 0.064 mA mM−1 sensitivity, and 0.19
mM detection limit. Under optimized transesteriﬁcation conditions (30 mg catalyst, 12 : 1 methanol/oil
ratio, 65 °C, and 210 min), a maximum biodiesel yield of 98.74% was obtained. The temperature
increases from 35 to 65 °C enhanced conversion, whereas the yield decreased to 90.50% at 75 °C.
Kinetic analysis favored the pseudo-ﬁrst-order model (R2 = 0.9612) over the pseudo-second-order
model (R2 = 0.5832). Thermodynamic parameters of DH° = 81.05 kJ mol−1, DS° = 261.04 J mol−1 K−1,
and DG° = −7.24 kJ mol−1 at 65 °C indicated an endothermic and favorable process. GC-MS conﬁrmed
FAME formation, with methyl stearate (40.59%), methyl oleate (27.81%), methyl linoleate (20.51%), and
methyl palmitate (9.83%) as the major components. The catalyst maintained 92.4% biodiesel yield after

1. Introduction
The depletion of fossil fuel resources and the increasing
concern for the environment and for reliable energy sources
have prompted the search for new materials to produce
renewable fuels and for electrochemical energy technologies.1
Biodiesel is one of the most appealing renewable fuels due to its
biodegradability, lower toxicity, and ability to be used in existing diesel engines, as well as the potential to produce biodiesel
from natural, non-edible oil and from waste. Waste cooking oil
(WCO) is an especially promising biodiesel feedstock because
its utilization simultaneously provides a low-cost raw material
and an eﬀective route for reducing waste disposal. However, the
transesterication of WCO is oen complicated by free fatty

environmental friendliness.4 The nitrogen-rich structure of gC3N4 has many active sites and makes it a suitable candidate to
provide surface sites relevant to heterogeneous catalysis and
electrochemical processes. However, the intrinsic surface area
and electrical conductivity of pristine g-C3N4 are relatively low,
which can restrict electron transport and the availability of

Rare-earth titanates have become attractive functional oxides
due to their structural stability, chemical durability, and
tunable physicochemical properties. In particular, Gd2TiO5
provides a metal-oxide component that can introduce additional surface-active sites and modify the electronic environment of a heterostructure. Nevertheless, oxide nanoparticles
can undergo aggregation, which may decrease the accessible
surface area and limit exposure of active sites. Integration with
suitable carbonaceous and semiconductor supports can therefore improve particle dispersion and strengthen interfacial
interactions.7,8
Graphene oxide (GO), characterized by its two-dimensional
layered structure, high surface area, oxygen-containing functional groups, and excellent mechanical stability, is a leading
carbon nanomaterial for multifunctional composite development.9,10 The hydroxyl, epoxy, and carboxyl functionalities of GO
can promote interactions with semiconductor and metal-oxide
species, thereby facilitating their dispersion and interfacial
coupling.11 In addition, the carbon framework can provide
interconnected pathways for charge transport and enhance the
accessibility of surface sites. These properties make GO
a promising material for improving electrochemical activity and
heterogeneous catalysis.12–15
The integration of GO, Gd2TiO5, and g-C3N4 into a ternary
nanocomposite is expected to generate signicant synergistic
eﬀects. In this heterostructure, g-C3N4 acts as a stable layered
matrix rich in nitrogen-active sites; Gd2TiO5 contributes catalytic redox centers, while GO forms a highly conductive network
that promotes rapid electron transport and suppresses nanoparticle agglomeration.16 The strong interfacial interactions
among these components are anticipated to increase the
density of accessible active sites, facilitate charge transfer,
improve electrochemical characteristics, and enhance catalytic
eﬃciency during biodiesel production. Such synergistic engineering provides a promising strategy for developing multifunctional materials capable of addressing both renewable fuel
production and electrochemical applications.17
In this study, a ternary nanocomposite composed of GOGd2TiO5 and g-C3N4 was synthesized using a hydrothermal
method. It was characterized in terms of its structural,
morphological, and chemical properties. The electrochemical
behavior of the nanocomposite was investigated through cyclic
voltammetry (CV), galvanostatic charge–discharge (GCD), and
electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS). Additionally, its
catalytic performance for biodiesel production from waste
cooking oil was evaluated by examining factors such as catalyst
dosage, methanol-to-oil ratio, reaction temperature, and

2.

Materials and methodology

Urea (CO(NH2)2, $99.5%), titanium tetrachloride (TiCl4, 99%),
graphite powder ($99%), potassium permanganate (KMnO4,
$99%), sulfuric acid (H2SO4, 98%), sodium nitrate (NaNO3,
$99%), hydrochloric acid (HCl, 37%), hydrogen peroxide
(H2O2, 30%), and absolute ethanol (C2H5OH, 99.9%) were
purchased from Sigma-Aldrich Co., LLC. Gadolinium(III) nitrate
(Gd(NO3)3$6H2O 99.9%) and concentrated nitric acid (HNO3,
68–70%) were obtained from Merck Co., LLC. Throughout all
experiments, distilled water (18.2 MU cm) was used.
2.1. Synthesis of g-C3N4
Thermal polymerization of urea was done to synthesize
Graphitic carbon nitride (g-C3N4). Briey, 30 g of urea was
transferred into a covered alumina crucible and heated to
550 °C for 4 hours in a muﬄe furnace. Aer naturally cooling,
the resulting yellow product was collected and ground into
a ne powder.18
2.2. Synthesis of Gd2TiO5
Gd2TiO5 was synthesized with the sol–gel assisted impregnation
method. First, 8 mL of ethanol and 10 mL of titanium tetrachloride were added gradually while being agitated. The
resulting mixture was aged for 24 hours to synthesized a yellow
homogeneous gel. The gel was subsequently dried at 120 °C for
3 hours and ground to get ne dried gel powder. The dried gel
was heated at 500 °C for 2 hours to obtain TiO2. This TiO2 was
impregnated with Gd(NO3)3$6H2O in an aqueous medium
where Gd2O3 was dissolved in concentrated HNO3. The
impregnated material was dried at 120 °C for 24 hours and
nally calcined at 500 °C for 3 hours to yield crystalline
Gd2TiO5.
2.3. Synthesis of graphene oxide (GO)
Graphene oxide (GO) was produced using a modied Hummers'
method. Specically, 1 g of graphite powder and 0.5 g of NaNO3
were added to 23 mL of 96% H2SO4 and stirred in a cooled water
coil at 5 °C. Aer stirring, 3 g of KMnO4 was added to the
previous mixture, and stirring was continued for an additional
2 h. The mixture was then heated to 35 °C, and 46 mL of
distilled water was added and maintained at 98 °C for 30 min.
In order to neutralize the reaction, an additional 140 mL of
distilled water and 10 mL of 10% H2O2 were added. The mixture
was then subjected to centrifugation to allow the GO product to
settle. The GO material was repeatedly washed until a neutral

3.

Results and discussion

3.1. X-ray diﬀraction (XRD) analysis
The patterns of XRD, pristine Gd2TiO5, Gd2TiO5/g-C3N4, and
GO–Gd2TiO5/g-C3N4 are shown in Fig. 1(A–C). The diﬀraction
peaks of pure Gd2TiO5 in Fig. 1(B) are sharp and well-dened,
at 2q = 25.8°, 36.8°, 48.2°, 54.3°, 56.2°, 62.8°, 70.4°, and
75.3°, which correspond to the characteristic planes of
orthorhombic Gd2TiO5, conrming the high crystallinity and
phase purity of pure Gd2TiO5. The characteristic (002)
diﬀraction peak of g-C3N4 at ∼27.3° appears in the XRD
patterns of the Gd2TiO5/g-C3N4 and GO-Gd2TiO5/g-C3N4
composites (Fig. 1(A)), while the weak (100) peak at ∼13° is
suppressed, revealing strong interactions between the
Gd2TiO5 and g-C3N4 matrix.20,21 Gd2TiO2's distinctive
diﬀraction peaks remain unchanged aer the composite is
created, suggesting that the crystal structure of Gd2TiO2 is
maintained.22–24 No distinct diﬀraction peak corresponding
to GO is observed near 10–11°, which is attributed to its low
loading, exfoliated structure, and homogeneous dispersion
within the composite.25
Fig. 1(C) shows the XRD pattern of graphene oxide (GO),
revealing a weak peak at approximately 26° and a broad,
prominent peak centered around 42–43° (2q). These diﬀraction
characteristics indicate a low level of crystallinity and suggest
structural disorder within the GO sheets. The absence of sharp,
well-dened diﬀraction peaks points to a limited long-range
structural ordering, likely due to defects and irregular stacking of the carbon layers. Consequently, the analyzed GO material appears to exhibit a predominantly disordered carbon
structure with some localized ordered domains.
Furthermore, the slight peak broadening and reduced
diﬀraction intensity of the GO-Gd2TiO5/g-C3N4 nanocomposite
indicate reduced crystallite size and stronger interfacial
coupling between GO, Gd2TiO5, and g-C3N4. These ndings
conrm the successful formation of a well-integrated ternary
heterostructure with preserved crystallinity. The XRD analysis
demonstrates the successful synthesis of GO-Gd2TiO5/g-C3N4
ternary nanocomposite without alteration of the crystalline
structure of Gd2TiO5. The eﬀective integration of GO and g-C3N4
with Gd2TiO5 is expected to improve electron transport and
provide enhanced structural stability for electrochemical and
catalytic applications.

3.2. Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) analysis
Fig. 2(D) presents the FTIR spectra of g-C3N4, GO-Gd2TiO5, and
the GO-Gd2TiO5/g-C3N4 ternary nanocomposite. All spectra
exhibit absorption features across a broad range. The g-C3N4
spectra show absorption at 3400 cm−1, attributed to residual –
OH and –NH groups, characterized by broad absorption in this
region. The bands at 1200–1650 cm−1 are assigned to the
stretching vibration of C–N and C]N bond in the heterocyclic
network, and the characteristic band at 810 cm−1 corresponds
to the out of plane bending of the tri-s-triazine ring units. The
polymeric structure of graphitic carbon nitride is featured by
these features.26,27 The FTIR spectrum of GO-Gd2TiO5 shows
absorption at 3400 cm−1 due to –OH groups. The bending
vibration of adsorbed water and contributions from the
graphitic C]C framework can give rise to bands that may be
observed in the range 1600–1650 cm−1, depending on the
spectrum. In addition, the C]O and C–O related vibrations
may be observed in the regions of 1700–1750 cm−1 and 1000–
1300 cm−1 in GO. Metal–oxygen vibrations associated with the
gadolinium–titanium oxide lattice is generally expected in the
lower-wavenumber region; however, their assignments require
conrmation from the original spectrum, particularly below
1000 cm−1.24,28,29 Aer the formation of the composite, GOGd2TiO5/g-C3N4 retains the primary vibrational features of GO,
Gd2TiO5, and g-C3N4. Minor changes in band positions and
intensities indicate interactions at the interfaces. These alterations suggest successful coupling of the individual components while preserving the integrity of the chemically bonded
structures.
The FTIR results conrm the successful formation of the GOGd2TiO5/g-C3N4 nanocomposite through the coexistence of
characteristic functional groups from all three components.
The observed spectral shis suggest strong interfacial interactions that are expected to increase the electrical conductivity,
charge transfer, and electrochemical performance and catalytic
activity of biodiesel production.
3.3. FT-Raman analysis
The investigation into the structural characteristics and vibrational properties of synthesized nanocomposites graphene
oxide (GO), g-C3N4, GO/g-C3N4, GO-Gd2TiO5, and GO-Gd2TiO5/
g-C3N4 was conducted using Raman spectroscopy. The Raman
spectrum of pristine GO reveals characteristic D and G bands at
approximately 1350 and 1590 cm−1, indicating structural
defects and graphitic domains via sp2-hybridized carbon. Pristine g-C3N4 shows vibrational bands across several ranges, with
signicant features around 480–745 cm−1 linked to ring deformation, and from 1145 to 1625 cm−1 attributed to C–N and
C]N stretching.30,31
In the GO/g-C3N4 composite, the D and G bands coexist with
features from the g-C3N4 framework, indicating an interaction
between these materials. The introduction of Gd2TiO5 results in
additional Raman features around 400–1000 cm−1, indicating
lattice vibrations from Gd–O and Ti–O bonds, while the carbonrelated bands remain intact, suggesting the graphitic framework's preservation.32

5.4. Mechanistic interpretation of biodiesel formation over
GO-Gd2TiO5/g-C3N4
The kinetic and thermodynamic ndings illuminate the catalytic mechanism in the GO-Gd2TiO5/g-C3N4 nanocomposite,
with the high eﬀective catalytic performance attributed to the
catalytic mechanism in the nanocomposite of GO-Gd2TiO5/gC3N4, and the high eﬀective catalytic performance can be
related to the synergistic inuence of the three components of
the ternary nanocomposite.
In transesterication, triglycerides and methanol interact with
the catalytic surface, and the functional and active sites of the
catalyst help facilitate the activation of the reactants. The oxygencontaining functionalities of GO can help enhance the adsorption
and interfacial interactions of the reactants, and the Gd2TiO5
provides the polar catalytic sites that facilitate the polarization of
the triglyceride ester groups. The g-C3N4 component plays a role
in enhancing the interfacial structure of the composite and
introduces nitrogen-containing surface functionalities.62
Methanol activation produces a more nucleophilic methoxide species:
CH3OH + Base # CH3O− + Base-H+

The carbonyl carbon of the triglyceride is subsequently
attacked nucleophilically by the activated methoxide species,
resulting in the formation of a tetrahedral intermediate. This
intermediate is then rearranged to yield a diglyceride intermediate and a fatty acid methyl ester (FAME). The reaction of
diglyceride and monoglyceride methanol yields more FAME and
glycerol:
Triglyceride + 3CH3OH / 3FAME + Glycerol

The GO-Gd2TiO5/g-C3N4 composite exhibited superior catalytic performance compared to the individual components due

to the multi-synergistic interface, which improved active site
accessibility as well as interactions between methanol and
triglycerides. Kinetic studies indicated that PFO model, was
a better t with the observed correlation coeﬃcient (R2) value of
0.9612 when compared to the PSO model R2 = 0.5832. The
Arrhenius analysis gave an activation energy (Ea) of
31.25 kJ mol−1, which indicated that the energy barrier for the
process of catalytic transesterication was of a moderate value.
The reaction was endothermic, as shown by the positive
enthalpy change (DH°) of 81.05 kJ mol−1. Additionally, the
system's disorder increased, indicated by the positive entropy
change (DS°) of +261.04 J mol−1 K−1. The negative value of (DG°)
of −7.22 kJ mol−1 at 65 °C implied that the reaction was thermodynamically favorable at the optimal conditions. The Van't
Hoﬀ thermodynamic study shows that the equilibrium constant
(K) increases as temperature rise due to endothermic reaction as
it needed heat to the system, which shis the equilibrium to
right to produce the more products which increase in equilibrium constant (K), so ln K increases. The composite GOGd2TiO5/g-C3N4 was an eﬀective catalyst system, producing
a maximum yield of 98.74% biodiesel at 65 °C, with 30 mg
catalyst, a 12 : 1 methanol/oil ratio, and a reaction time of
210 min. Increasing the temperature to 75 °C resulted in
a decreased yield, suggesting that methanol was being lost from
the reaction mixture and undergoing changes.

Conclusion

A ternary GO-Gd2TiO5/g-C3N4 nanocomposite was successfully
developed for WCO-to-biodiesel conversion, exhibiting superior
activity compared with GO, g-C3N4, and GO-Gd2TiO5. Under
optimized conditions (30 mg catalyst, 12 : 1 methanol/oil, 65 °C,
210 min), a maximum biodiesel yield of 98.74% was achieved.
The reaction followed apparent pseudo-rst-order kinetics, with
R2 = 0.9612, substantially higher than the pseudo-second-order
model (R2 = 0.5832). Thermodynamic parameters (DH° =
81.05 kJ mol−1, DS° = 261.04 J mol−1 K−1, and DG° =
−7.24 kJ mol−1 at 65 °C) conrmed the endothermic and
thermodynamically favorable nature of the process. GC-MS
identied methyl stearate (40.59%), methyl oleate (27.81%),
methyl linoleate (20.51%), and methyl palmitate (9.83%) as the
major FAMEs. The electrochemical results further demonstrated good functional stability, with 97% capacitance retention aer 7000 cycles, symmetrical GCD proles, a 0–100 mM
linear response, 0.064 mA mM−1 sensitivity, and 0.19 mM detection limit. Catalytically, the material retained 92.4% biodiesel
yield aer ve cycles, corresponding to 93.58% activity retention. Overall, the combination of 98.74% biodiesel yield,
favorable kinetics and thermodynamics, high electrochemical
cycling stability, and good recyclability demonstrates the
potential of GO-Gd2TiO5/g-C3N4 as a multifunctional nanocomposite. Future studies should address long-term cycling
beyond ve catalytic cycles, catalyst regeneration, metal leaching, continuous-ow operation, scale-up, and techno-economic
