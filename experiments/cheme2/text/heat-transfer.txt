ABSTRACT: Shell-and-tube heat exchangers are widely used in
industrial processes, but improving the shell-side heat transfer
without causing excessive pressure losses remains a major
engineering challenge. Numerical simulations provide an efficient
approach for evaluating thermohydraulic enhancement techniques
while reducing experimental costs. The present study evaluates the
heat transfer in a shell-and-tube heat exchanger using computational fluid dynamics by means of ANSYS Fluent software. Unlike
previous numerical studies that mainly focused on thermal
enhancement, this work provides a comprehensive thermohydraulic assessment by simultaneously evaluating heat-transfer improvement together with the associated pressure-drop and pumpingpower penalties over different nanoparticle concentrations and mass flow rates. Therefore, a simplified heat-exchanger model was
developed with six baffles operating under turbulent flow conditions. The working fluid in the shell is initially water, which is
subsequently replaced by nanofluid composed of water and 15 nm Al2O3 nanoparticles, while the inner tube walls are kept at a
constant temperature. The results show that the Al2O3/water nanofluid significantly enhances the heat transfer. Thermal
conductivity and nanoparticle concentrations play a crucial role in this enhancement. The largest increase in heat transfer, 37.83%,
occurs at a nanoparticle concentration of 1.25% at mass flow rates of 0.5 kg/s, 0.6 kg/s, and 0.7 kg/s. Lower concentrations result in
30.76% (concentration of 1.00%), 23.32% (concentration of 0.75%), 15.47% (concentration of 0.50%), and 7.16% (concentration of
0.25%) improvements compared to water. Similarly, the heat transfer coefficient increases by 47.84%, 38.32%, 28.62%, 18.70%, and
8.51%, for concentrations of 1.25%, 1.00%, 0.75%, 0.50%, and 0.25%, respectively. However, the use of nanofluids leads to an
increase in the pressure drop, with maximum values reaching 5.30%. The results demonstrate that dilute Al2O3/water nanofluids can
significantly enhance shell-side thermal performance while maintaining relatively low pressure drop and pumping power penalties,
highlighting their potential as a practical thermo-hydraulic enhancement strategy for shell-and-tube heat exchangers and providing
useful guidance for the design and optimization of industrial heat exchangers.

1. INTRODUCTION
In the past decade, increasing energy, the depletion of fossil
fuels (such as oil, coal, and natural gas), climate change,
atmospheric pollution, and the energy crisis have driven
significant research on optimizing energy use to enhance
efficiency and lower losses.1,2 One of the principal approaches
involves improving heat-transfer technologies.3
Heat exchangers are essential components in energy transfer
and conversion systems,4 widely used in industries such as oil
and gas,5 manufacturing,6 petrochemicals,7 pharmaceuticals,8
HVAC,9 and refrigeration.10 Among them, the shell-and-tube
heat exchanger is the most commonly used type.11
The shell-and-tube heat exchangers are commonly carried
out in accordance with the standards established by the
Tubular Exchanger Manufacturers Association (TEMA).12 For
shell-side flow analysis, correlations such as the Kern method13

According to Nakhchi and Esfahani,17 heat exchangers are
essential devices for achieving energy savings in industrial
processes. On the other hand, the thermophysical properties of
the fluids commonly used in this equipment for heat transfer
purposes are some of the fundamental factors that influence
the heat exchange capacity of these fluids, which, in turn, are
not always within the desired parameters.18 Therefore, given
the constraints related to the dimensions, market value, and
thermophysical properties of working fluids, it is essential to
improve the performance of existing heat exchangers and to
design new units that are lighter, more compact, and,
consequently, less expensive.19
In order to overcome this condition and increase heat
transfer, it is possible to change the boundary conditions, make
geometric modifications to the equipment, or apply external
agents. 20 One of the most widespread methods for
implementing the last alternative is to improve the
thermophysical properties of the working fluid by adding
nanoparticles. The result of this mixture is called a nanofluid.21
Nanofluids are composed of solid particles of metallic type,
metal oxides, carbon-based nanomaterials such as carbon
nanotubes, CNTs, or nonmetallic oxides, of nanometric size
(less than 100 nm in diameter), which are dispersed in fluids
used as coolants, such as water, ethylene glycol, propylene
glycol, R600 refrigerant, or oil.22 This new fluid is used to
improve the performance of thermal devices, such as heat
exchangers,21,23−26 without altering their primary geometry.27−29 This use is justified by the low thermal conductivity
of traditional refrigerants.30
The principal reason for this improvement in thermal energy
exchange capacity is that this mixture has a combined effect of
the properties of the base fluid and the nanoparticles.31
However, the effectiveness of nanofluids in promoting heat
transfer can also be influenced by other factors, such as gravity,
Brownian motion, Brownian diffusion, the friction force
between the fluid and the nanoparticles, sedimentation,
dispersion, and stratification at the solid/liquid interface;
these factors may simultaneously interact within the main flow
of this fluid.18 Consequently, several studies have focused on
identifying the mechanisms that influence heat transfer in
nanofluids while demonstrating that an improvement in
thermal conductivity alone is not sufficient to explain the
performance gains observed in the process.32
Nanofluids formulated with Al2O3 nanoparticles have
attracted significant attention due to their superior thermal
characteristics, high productivity, and stability.33 For instance,
Main et al.23 investigated the thermal conductivity of
trifluoromethylsulfonyl (as base fluid) and the thermal
conductivity after the addition of Al2O3 nanoparticles at a
1.0% volumetric concentration, diameters of 10 nm, 30, 60,
and 90 nm and varying temperatures (10 °C - 70 °C). The
results showed that thermal conductivity increased by up to
14.63% when nanoparticles with a diameter of 10 nm were
added at 70 °C.
Albadr et al.34 investigated the heat transfer performance of
a shell-and-tube heat exchanger operating under countercurrent and turbulent flow conditions with Al2O3/distilled
water nanofluids. Their findings indicated that dispersing
nanoparticles into distilled water led to an enhancement in
both the thermal conductivity and viscosity of the suspension,
with the latter contributing significantly to the pressure drop.

In addition, it was observed that the magnitudes of these
effects increased with particle concentration. Moreover, when
the nanoparticle concentration reached 2% by volume, the
overall heat transfer coefficient improved by up to 57%
compared to that of pure distilled water.
Another relevant contribution was presented by Oztürk,
Tezcan, and Ç iftçi,35 who performed a computational analysis
on heat transfer enhancement in a shell-and-tube heat
exchanger using Al2O3/water and CNT/water nanofluids at
concentrations of 3%, 6%, and 9%. As expected, heat transfer
increased with nanoparticle concentration. The maximum
enhancement compared with pure water was approximately
50% and 62.5% at a concentration of 6% for Al2O3/water and
CNT/water nanofluids, respectively. Although improvements
were also observed at 9% concentration, the enhancement was
reduced due to the dominant effect of viscosity at higher
nanoparticle loadings, which negatively affects heat transfer, as
reported in previous studies.36
Similarly, Gkountas et al.37 evaluated heat transfer enhancement in a supercritical CO2 Brayton cycle printed circuit heat
exchanger using an Al2O3/water nanofluid. The results showed
that a maximum volume fraction of 5% nanoparticles resulted
in a 10% improvement in the heat-transfer coefficient
compared to pure water. This improvement allowed for an
additional 0.9% reduction in the length of the heat exchanger,
along with a decrease in the pressure drop of up to 14%.
The Al2O3/water nanofluid has attracted the attention of the
academic community and the industrial sector because of its
innovative properties.21 According to the literature review,
several studies have focused on evaluating the heat transfer
enhancement in a shell-and-tube heat exchanger through the
use of this nanofluid. These studies show that volumetric
concentrations of Al2O3/water greater than 1% have been
employed in this type of equipment, showing positive results in
the heat transfer process but with higher pressure drop
penalties. However, few studies address the use of Al2O3/water
nanofluid circulating on the shell side of shell-and-tube heat
exchanger at lower concentrations, as shown in Table 1.
Furthermore, although numerous studies have investigated
nanofluids in heat exchangers, many have focused primarily on
maximizing heat transfer enhancement without sufficiently
addressing the thermohydraulic trade-off associated with
increased pressure drop and pumping power requirements.
Therefore, the present study focuses on evaluating the
thermohydraulic performance of low-concentration Al2O3/
water nanofluids under shell-side turbulent flow conditions,
emphasizing the balance between heat transfer enhancement
and hydraulic penalties in terms of pressure drop and pumping
power requirements.
The novelty of the present work lies in the thermohydraulic
evaluation of dilute Al2O3/water nanofluids with volume
concentrations ranging from 0.25% to 1.25% under shell-side
turbulent flow conditions in a shell-and-tube heat exchanger.
Unlike many previous studies that primarily focused on heat
transfer enhancement at higher nanoparticle concentrations,
the present investigation jointly assesses thermal and hydraulic
performance through the heat transfer rate, heat transfer
coefficient, pressure drop, and pumping power. This approach
provides practical design-oriented information regarding the
feasibility of employing low-concentration nanofluids in
industrial shell and tube heat exchangers.
For this purpose, a shell-and-tube heat exchanger operating
with water and subsequently with Al2O3/water nanofluids at

addressed in this study are governed by the fundamental
conservation laws of mass (eqs 1), linear momentum (eqs 2),
and energy (eqs 3). These equations, which represent the
physical principles of conservation, are employed to evaluate

time. A steady-state approach was adopted because the present
study focuses on evaluating the average performance of the

simulations, the inlet mass flow rate, inlet temperature, and
wall temperature were maintained constant, with no transient
thermal or hydrodynamic disturbances imposed on the system.
In addition, the following assumptions are adopted for the case
analyzed in this study:
• The nanofluid is considered homogeneous; according to
Garoosi et al.,82 this is valid for nanoparticle volume
concentrations below 5%.
• The nanofluid is modeled as a Newtonian fluid; as
indicated by Chen et al.,83 this approximation is
appropriate for nanoparticle concentrations under 5%.
• The thermophysical properties of both the base fluid and
the nanoparticles are assumed to be constant, uniform,
and isotropic.
• The base fluid and the nanoparticles are assumed to be
in hydrodynamic equilibrium.
• Mass and interfacial motion are neglected.
• Nondrag interfacial forces, such as lift forces, wall
lubrication effects, and virtual mass, are disregarded.
Furthermore, this study focused exclusively on the influence
of nanoparticle concentration on the performance of the shell
and tube heat exchanger. Other important factors affecting
nanofluid behavior, such as nanoparticle size, dispersion
stability, sedimentation, and agglomeration effects, were not
explicitly modeled or analyzed. For instance, smaller nanoparticles may enhance the thermal properties of nanofluids and
consequently improve heat transfer.24 In contrast, larger
nanoparticles, higher pressure drops, and increased pumping
power represent important challenges for future research.84,85
However, the investigated concentration range was limited
to dilute conditions to minimize excessive agglomeration and
stability-related effects commonly observed at higher nanoparticle concentrations.
It should be noted that the mixture model adopted in this
study provides a computationally efficient framework for
simulating dilute nanofluid suspensions. Nevertheless, this
approach assumes local equilibrium between the base fluid and
nanoparticles and does not explicitly capture complex
phenomena such as particle agglomeration, sedimentation
dynamics, or other concentration-dependent interactions that
may become significant at higher nanoparticle loadings.
Consequently, the present results should be interpreted within
the scope of dilute and relatively stable nanofluid suspensions.

simulating nanofluid flows with the multiphase model. Secondorder schemes were also adopted for momentum, energy,
turbulent kinetic energy, and turbulent energy dissipation rate.
In contrast, the volume fraction of the secondary phase was
discretized using a first-order upwind scheme. These
discretization schemes were chosen due to their simplicity,
rapid convergence, improved accuracy, and lower susceptibility
to numerical oscillations.92 Since there is currently no
universally accepted convergence criterion for numerical flow
simulations,93 residual monitoring is commonly adopted as a
reliable indicator during the iterative solution process. In the
present study, convergence criteria of 10−3 were imposed for
the continuity, momentum, turbulent kinetic energy, turbulent
dissipation rate, and nanoparticle volume fraction equations
(when operating with nanofluid), while a stricter criterion of
10−6 was applied to the energy equation.
In addition to residual monitoring, the convergence of the
simulations was verified through the stabilization of global
thermohydraulic parameters, including the shell-side outlet
temperature, total heat transfer rate, and pressure drop, which
exhibited negligible variation with further iterations.

generation analysis has been increasingly recognized as an

enhancement techniques. Therefore, future studies should
incorporate local and global entropy generation rates to
identify the optimum nanoparticle concentration and operating
conditions from a second law perspective. This approach has
been emphasized in recent studies on enhanced heat-transfer
systems and nanofluid-based thermal devices.117,118

geometric features and flow development at the system entry.
Upon entering, the fluid encounters resistances that hinder its
progression through the equipment, primarily driven by flowdirection changes, recirculations, and wall friction. The latter is
slightly augmented with increasing Al2O3 nanoparticle
concentrations due to the associated rise in fluid viscosity.122
The high velocity at which the flow enters makes it possible to
overcome these resistances, making it possible for it to flow
naturally through the equipment, however, with a higher
penalty in pressure drop, as illustrated in Figure 13.
In the regions where recirculations occur (see Figure 8), the
flow becomes more turbulent, and high local pressures are
observed due to fluid impingement against the solid surfaces of
the device, leading to the dissipation of kinetic energy.
Consequently, this continuous degradation of mechanical
energy results in a progressive reduction in static pressure
along the equipment, as shown in Figure 13.

by the concept of specific heat, also referred to as specific heat
capacity, which is a physical property that represents the
amount of heat required to raise the temperature of a unit of
mass of a substance by one degree. In this context, it is
expected that the use of nanofluids to enhance heat transfer
performance leads to a reduction in specific heat. This occurs

because the high thermal conductivity introduced by the
addition of solid nanoparticles to the base fluid enables a more
efficient heat conduction. Consequently, the specific heat
capacity of the nanofluid is reduced, which implies that a lower
amount of energy is required to increase its temperature.124
This behavior is consistent with that reported by Cruz et al.,68
who studied the application of nanofluids in shell-and-tube
heat exchangers through CFD analysis.

4. CONCLUSIONS
This work evaluated the impact of using Al2O3/water
nanofluids on enhancing the heat-transfer rate in a shell-andtube heat exchanger. It can be concluded that correlationbased approaches show an adequate agreement with the CFD
results. While these correlations may suggest potential design
deficiencies, CFD simulations enable the identification of their
origins and locations. Furthermore, the Kern method systematically underestimates the heat transfer coefficient values,
resulting in a larger percentage deviation compared with the
CFD results. For this reason, the baseline comparisons (using
water alone) were carried out using the Bell-Delaware method,
which incorporates correction factors and therefore provides
greater accuracy.
CFD analyses demonstrated that the addition of nanoparticles to the base fluid (water) significantly enhanced heat
transfer within the heat exchanger due to their superior
thermophysical properties, showing consistency with the
findings reported in the literature. Specifically, the heat transfer
rate increased by 7.16% at a nanoparticle volume concen-

constraints of the inlet section. Furthermore, the number
and configuration of the baffles contribute significantly to the

various geometric modifications as strategies to reduce the
associated pressure drop. It was also verified that the pressure
drop increases accordingly with increasing mass flow rate and
nanoparticle concentration. This behavior is attributed to the
intensification of turbulence driven by higher flow rates and
the increase in fluid viscosity caused by the presence of the
nanoparticles.
In addition, a regression model based on the CFD
simulation results was proposed. It was observed that the Rsquared value of the resulting model is 0.9998, indicating a
high level of accuracy and reliability for the proposed
regression under the geometry adopted in this study.
From an engineering perspective, the results demonstrated
that dilute Al2O3/water nanofluids can enhance shell-side
thermal performance while maintaining relatively low pressure
drop and pumping power penalties. From a practical design
perspective, low nanoparticle concentrations may therefore
represent a more feasible operating condition for shell-andtube heat exchangers than higher concentrations, which are
commonly associated with an excessive viscosity increase and
hydraulic resistance. The main contribution of this work lies in
the combined evaluation of heat-transfer enhancement,
pressure drop, and pumping power, demonstrating that lowconcentration Al2O3/water nanofluids provide a favorable
balance between thermal performance and hydraulic penalties
under shell-side operating conditions. This integrated
thermohydraulic assessment offers a more practical basis for
the design and optimization of shell-and-tube heat exchangers
than heat-transfer analysis alone. Future studies should extend
the present work through experimental validation, entropygeneration analysis based on the second law of thermodynamics, the evaluation of alternative heat-exchanger geometries
