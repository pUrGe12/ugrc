ABSTRACT: Considering the escalating severity of global climate
warming, reducing CO2 emissions has gained widespread
recognition among the international community. Post-combustion
capture is one of the most widely adopted technologies in this field.
Chemical absorption, though mature, suffers from inherent
drawbacks such as high regeneration energy consumption and
significant solvent loss. Therefore, novel high-efficiency absorbents
are urgently needed. In this study, an amino dual-functionalized
ionic liquid [TEPAH][ATA] was synthesized via a one-step
method and then mixed with NPA and water to construct a ternary
phase-change absorption system, [TEPAH][ATA]/NPA/H2O.
The CO2 absorption-desorption performance, phase-change
behavior, and reaction mechanism of this system were systematically investigated. Results showed that the CO2 absorption loading reached 1.79 mol CO2 mol−1 IL. Upon saturation, the system
spontaneously separated into two liquid phases, where the rich phase accounted for only 28% of the total volume yet contained over
96% of the absorbed CO2, and its viscosity was 6.59 mPa s. After five absorption-desorption cycles, the regeneration efficiency
remained at 88.6%, demonstrating good cyclic stability. This study provides a reference for the application of ionic liquids in CO2
capture.

1. INTRODUCTION
Carbon dioxide emissions from human activities are the
primary driver of the rapid increase in atmospheric CO2
concentrations.1 These emissions also constitute a major
cause of severe environmental issues such as global climate
change and glacial melting.2 Given that fossil fuels are expected
to remain central to global economic development as well as
modern social operations for the foreseeable future, the
comprehensive deployment of CCUS technology is of
paramount importance.3 Currently, CCUS is recognized as
an effective pathway for deep CO2 emission reduction,
encompassing three key stages: carbon capture, utilization,
and storage.4,5
Among existing carbon capture technologies, post-combustion capture is one of the most widely adopted and
technologically mature options, particularly suitable for
retrofitting existing industrial sources such as coal-fired
power plants, cement kilns, and steel mills.6 Available methods
include adsorption, absorption, membrane separation, and
cryogenic separation.7,8 Among them, chemical absorption
using monoethanolamine (MEA) remains the industrial
benchmark due to its superior absorption rate, achieving a
loading capacity of 0.5 mol CO2/mol MEA.9 However, this
method suffers from significant drawbacks, including severe
equipment corrosion and high regeneration energy consump© 2026 The Authors. Published by

The structural designability of ionic liquids offers vast
potential for functionalized modification.20 By regulating ionic
structures or introducing specific functional groups, researchers
can obtain functionalized ionic liquids with tailored properties.
Numerous studies confirm that primary and secondary amines,
acting as active centers, significantly enhance the CO2
absorption capacity and selectivity of ionic liquids.20 Inspired
by these findings, researchers have synthesized a series of novel
amino-functionalized ionic liquids by grafting amino groups
onto ionic liquid structural units through molecular modification. Chen et al. designed and synthesized four aminofunctionalized ionic liquids for capturing low-concentration
CO2. All four exhibited excellent CO2 absorption performance
at room temperature, with [TETA][HCOO] showing the best
performance.21 Its decarbonization rate reached as high as
98%. The significance of this pioneering achievement is
threefold: combining the advantages of chemical absorption
with the designability of ionic liquids, providing a model for
subsequent research on functionalized ionic liquids for CO2
capture, and laying a solid theoretical and experimental
foundation.
Phase-change absorption is a novel CO2 capture technology
that has received widespread attention in recent years.22 Its
core mechanism lies in the phase separation of the absorbent
after CO2 absorption, forming a CO2-rich phase and a CO2lean phase, with most CO2 absorption products aggregating in
the rich phase.23 Therefore, only the rich phase requires
regeneration during the process, a strategy that significantly
reduces regeneration energy consumption.24 Liquid−liquid
phase-change absorbents developed from organic amines can
substantially lower energy requirements. Research indicates
that the regeneration energy of phase-change absorbents can
be substantially reduced compared to the traditional MEA
process, demonstrating immense application potential. Zheng
et al. innovatively proposed an [IPDAH][Im] functionalized
ionic liquid, in which CO2 products enrich into the lower layer
and form a white crystalline precipitate after saturated
absorption.25 The volume of this phase accounts for only
60% of the total volume, and its regeneration energy is only
1.23 GJ/t CO2, far lower than that of MEA. Su et al. combined
triethylenetetramine and bromine as an ionic liquid with
alcohol and water to develop a liquid−liquid phase-change
absorbent.26 Compared to solid−liquid absorbents, this system
achieves phase separation faster and more simply, reaching an
absorption loading of 1.33 mol/mol upon saturation. The rich
phase absorbs 96.32% of the CO2 using only 35.3% of the total
volume, which greatly reduces regeneration energy consumption. Phase-change absorbents thus exhibit significant
application potential in the field of CO2 capture.
Despite these advances, many existing phase-change
absorbents still have notable drawbacks. Conventional aminebased biphasic systems typically suffer from high rich-phase
viscosity, often exceeding 20−50 mPa s.13 This is mainly
caused by the formation of extensive hydrogen-bonding
networks among carbamate species. The high viscosity severely
hinders mass transfer and raises pumping energy consumption.
To address these limitations, we adopt a dual-functionalization strategy. In this design, both cation and anion are
engineered to actively contribute to the capture and release of
CO2. The cation [TEPAH]+ contains multiple primary and

Article

secondary amine groups, providing abundant reactive sites for
high CO2 loading. The anion [ATA]‑, which bears an azole
ring and an amino group, participates not only in CO2 binding
but also in absorption enhancement. More importantly, it
promotes carbamate hydrolysis during desorption, thereby
reducing the energy barrier for regeneration.
Based on the research background, this paper synthesizes an
amino-difunctionalized ionic liquid using tetraethylenepentamine (TEPA) and 3-amino-1,2,4-triazole (ATA) as the
primary absorbents. The abundant amino sites in these
compounds enhance CO2 absorption performance.27,28 Water
and NPA are employed as a mixed solvent, with NPA also
serving to effectively regulate phase separation behavior.29 A
novel liquid−liquid phase-change ionic liquid absorption
system, [TEPAH][ATA]/NPA/H2O, is constructed. This
system aims to achieve synergistic optimization of three key
aspects: high-efficiency CO2 capture, low viscosity, and low
regeneration energy consumption.

2. EXPERIMENTAL SECTION
2.1. Chemicals
Tetraethylenepentamine (TEPA, 99% purity), 3-amino-1,2,4-triazole
(ATA, 99.93% purity), n-propanol (NPA, 99.7% purity), and ethanol
(EtOH, analytical grade) were obtained from Shanghai Aladdin BioChem Technology Co., Ltd. Carbon dioxide gas (99.99% purity) was
supplied by Guizhou Sanhe Gas Technology Co., Ltd., China.
Deionized water was used to prepare all solutions. All chemicals were
used as received without further purification.

2.2. Synthesis of [TEPAH][ATA]
The amino-functionalized ionic liquid [TEPAH][ATA] was prepared
by a one-step synthesis. In a typical procedure, 0.0125 mol of TEPA
and 0.0125 mol of ATA were dissolved in 30 mL of ethanol/water
(4:1 v/v) solution. The mixture was magnetically stirred at room
temperature for 12 h, then rotary evaporated at 60 °C under vacuum
to remove excess solvent. The product was obtained as a pale yellow
viscous liquid, which was the target ionic liquid [TEPAH][ATA]. The
product was then placed in a volumetric flask and diluted with NPA/
water mixtures of varying volume fractions to yield [TEPAH][ATA]/
NPA/water solutions with a concentration of 0.5 mol/L.

2.3. Experimental Procedure
2.3.1. Absorption Section. The absorbent solution was loaded
into a bubbling absorption tube. CO2 was continuously introduced at
a flow rate of 60 mL min−1, and the water bath was maintained at the
designated temperature. The inlet gas flow was regulated by a mass
flow controller, while the outlet gas flow rate was measured
periodically with a soap film flowmeter. The absorption process was
deemed complete when the outlet flow rate matched the inlet flow
rate and remained constant for 3 min, indicating saturation of the
solution. Upon saturation, the absorbent gradually separated into two
phases.
2.3.2. Desorption Section. The CO2-rich phase was transferred
to a bubbling absorption tube, immersed in an oil bath, and
magnetically stirred at a controlled temperature to facilitate
desorption. The outlet flow rate was also measured periodically
using the soap film flowmeter. After desorption, the rich phase was
mixed with the lean phase, and the above absorption procedure was
repeated to determine the CO2 loading capacity of the absorbent for
the next cycle.
The absorption mechanism of the ionic liquid was investigated by
13
C NMR spectroscopy, and its thermal stability was evaluated by
thermogravimetric analysis.

[TEPAH][ATA]/H2O binary system absorbed CO2 normally

Subsequent experimental analysis revealed that the upper
and lower layers correspond to an NPA-rich lean phase and an
absorbent-rich concentrated phase, respectively. Figure 4a
shows the variation of the rich phase volume fraction with the
initial NPA volume fraction. As the NPA content increased,
the rich phase volume gradually decreased. However, at
excessively high NPA concentrations, the rich phase volume
became extremely small and almost negligible, which adversely
affected the CO2 loading. These findings indicate that NPA
serves as a phase regulator in the system, and the upper phase
is predominantly composed of NPA.
Viscosity plays a crucial role in the CO2 absorption process,
as it strongly influences the diffusion and mass transfer of CO2
within the solution, thus affecting both absorption and
desorption performance. Figure 4b presents the viscosities of
the [TEPAH][ATA]/NPA/H2O solution measured at different temperatures. The viscosity of each phase in the system
was found to decrease sharply with rising temperature.
To demonstrate the role of amino functionalization, we
compared the CO2 absorption performance of [TEPAH][ATA] with that of [DETA][ATA] under identical conditions.
As shown in Figure 5, the [TEPAH][ATA] system exhibited a
significantly higher CO2 loading of 1.79 mol mol−1, compared
with 0.98 mol mol−1 for the [DETA][ATA] system. The
absorption rate of [TEPAH][ATA] was also higher throughout
the entire absorption period. These results confirm that a
greater number of amino groups directly contributes to higher
CO2 uptake, as more reactive sites are available for carbamate
formation.

optimal absorption performance was obtained at an NPA
volume fraction of 60% and a temperature of 303.15 K. At this
optimal condition, the absorption loading reached 1.79 mol
mol−1, the rich phase volume fraction was 28%, and the rich
phase viscosity was only 6.59 mPa s, which is considerably
lower than that of many conventional ionic liquid systems. A
comparison of the viscosity and absorption loading between

samples taken at various absorption stages, and the obtained

ppm are ascribed to the three carbon atoms of NPA. After CO2
saturation, the system underwent phase separation into two
liquid layers. The rich phase retained all the characteristic
peaks of the ionic liquid, and new signals appeared in the 163−
165 ppm region, corresponding to the carboxyl carbon (C�

3.4.2. Absorption and Desorption Mechanism. Based
on the experimental results and 13C NMR analysis above, the
CO2 absorption mechanism and phase separation behavior of
the [TEPAH][ATA]/NPA/H2O system are proposed as
follows. The absorbent contains a dual-functionalized ionic
liquid, [TEPAH][ATA], which possesses multiple primary and
secondary amine active sites, each with a high capacity for CO2

4. CONCLUSIONS
Overall, we synthesized a new ionic liquid [TEPAH][ATA]
and used it to build a high-performance [TEPAH][ATA]/
NPA/H2O ternary absorbent. Experimental results demonstrated that at an NPA volume fraction of 60% and a
temperature of 303.15 K, the system attained an optimal CO2
loading of 1.79 mol CO2 mol−1 ILs. Notably, the system
exhibited pronounced phase separation, with the CO2-rich
phase comprising only 28% of the total volume yet
concentrating over 96% of the absorbed CO2. This significant
enrichment of CO2 in a reduced phase volume greatly lowers
the thermal energy demand for regeneration. Moreover, the
saturated rich phase displayed a low viscosity of 6.59 mPa s,
