                                          Immobilizing visible-light-responsive photocatalysts within recoverable porous architectures requires
                                          a balance among active-phase retention, interfacial accessibility, and photon exposure. Here, Cu2O–Ag-
                                          containing domains were immobilized on a three-dimensional loofah scaﬀold through an acrylic-
                                          assisted strategy to construct a recoverable Cu2O–Ag@ACR/LF-3D monolith integrating a lignocellulosic
                                          sorptive framework, a polymeric immobilization interface, and visible-light-responsive domains. FTIR,
                                          XRD, SEM-EDX mapping, and UV-vis diﬀuse reﬂectance analyses conﬁrmed incorporation of the acrylic
                                          phase, retention of crystalline Cu2O, spatial distribution of Cu- and Ag-containing domains, and an
                                          apparent optical band gap of 1.99 eV. Control experiments showed that the pronounced dye removal
                                          was associated predominantly with the intact monolith rather than adsorption, direct photolysis, or
                                          leached species alone. Response surface methodology identiﬁed optimum conditions at pH 5.94, an
                                          initial dye concentration of 68.07 mg L−1, and a monolith dosage of 0.71 g, yielding a predicted
                                          decolorization eﬃciency of 98.14% and an experimentally validated value of 96.43%. TOC decreased by
                                          approximately 62% after 180 min, indicating substantial but incomplete mineralization, while reactive-
                                          species trapping supported the participation of cO2−, h+, and cOH, with stronger contributions from cO2−
