ABSTRACT: In this study, a delay-induced mathematical model
for biodiesel production is proposed by introducing a discrete time
delay into a reaction kinetic framework. The delay represents the
intrinsic lag associated with intermediate biochemical processes
during biodiesel synthesis. An optimal control strategy based on
ultrasound-assisted production is further incorporated to regulate
biodiesel yield while minimizing control cost. The resulting delayed
optimal control system is analyzed using Pontryagin’s Maximum
Principle. To efficiently simulate the delayed dynamics, a fixed-step
RK4 solver for delay differential equations is developed and
validated against the ddeint method. Numerical simulations
demonstrate that delay significantly affects transient biodiesel
dynamics and may induce oscillatory behavior. Sensitivity and
uncertainty analyses identify key kinetic parameters governing oscillation amplitude and system robustness. The results highlight the
potential of ultrasound-assisted control strategies for enhancing biodiesel production.

In recent years, mathematical modeling has emerged as a
powerful approach for understanding and optimizing biodiesel
production systems. Mathematical models allow researchers to
analyze reaction kinetics, investigate system stability, and
explore the influence of key parameters on biodiesel yield.
Various modeling approaches, including regression models,
polynomial approximations, and nonlinear differential equation
systems, have been employed to describe biodiesel production
dynamics.8,9 In particular, ordinary differential equation (ODE)
models have been used to represent the interaction between
triglycerides, diglycerides, monoglycerides, methanol, and
catalysts during the transesterification process.10 These models
provide valuable insights into the dynamic behavior of biodiesel
production systems and enable the identification of optimal
operating conditions.

INTRODUCTION
The rapid depletion of fossil fuel reserves and the increasing
environmental concerns associated with greenhouse gas
emissions have intensified the search for sustainable and
renewable energy sources. Among the various alternatives,
biodiesel has attracted considerable attention due to its
biodegradability, lower toxicity, and compatibility with existing
diesel engines. Biodiesel is commonly produced from nonedible
vegetable oil from the fruit of Jatropha Curcas plant, animal fats,
or waste cooking oils through a catalytic transesterification
process, in which triglycerides react with alcohol in the presence
of a catalyst to form fatty acid methyl esters (FAME) and
glycerol.1,2 Because of its favorable environmental profile and
renewable nature, biodiesel has been widely studied as a
promising substitute for conventional petroleum-based diesel
fuels.
The efficiency of biodiesel production strongly depends on
several physicochemical parameters such as temperature,
catalyst concentration, molar ratio of alcohol to oil, reaction
time, and mixing conditions.3,4 Numerous experimental studies
have investigated the influence of these parameters on biodiesel
yield and reaction kinetics. For example, temperature-dependent transesterification processes have been explored to enhance
biodiesel yield and reaction efficiency.5 Other studies have
examined the thermophysical properties of biodiesel−diesel
mixtures and developed empirical models to predict their

Despite the progress achieved in existing modeling studies,
most available mathematical models assume instantaneous
reaction responses and neglect the inherent time delays
associated with intermediate reaction stages. In practical
biodiesel production processes, however, delays may arise due
to several factors, including intermediate chemical transformations, catalyst activation periods, diffusion effects, and
mixing limitations within the reaction medium. Such delays
introduce memory effects into the system, meaning that the
current production rate may depend not only on the present
state variables but also on their past values. These phenomena
cannot be adequately captured by classical ODE models.
Delay differential equations (DDEs) provide a natural
mathematical framework for representing such processes with
memory. In delay models, the rate of change of a state variable
depends on its value at a previous time, thereby incorporating
the effects of reaction lag or intermediate biochemical
processes.17 In the context of biodiesel production, a delay
may arise in the formation of biodiesel from intermediate
compounds such as diglycerides and monoglycerides, or in the
activation dynamics of catalysts involved in the transesterification process. Consequently, incorporating delay into
biodiesel reaction models may provide a more realistic
description of the underlying kinetic mechanisms.
Another important aspect of biodiesel production is the use of
technological interventions to enhance reaction efficiency.
Ultrasound-assisted transesterification has been recognized as
an effective technique for accelerating biodiesel production by
improving mass transfer and reducing reaction time.18 However,
the application of such techniques involves operational costs,
making it desirable to determine optimal strategies that balance
production efficiency and energy expenditure.19 Optimal
control theory provides a systematic framework for determining
such strategies by identifying control inputs that maximize
desired outcomes while minimizing associated costs.
Motivated by these considerations, the present study
proposes a delay-induced mathematical model for biodiesel
production by incorporating a discrete time delay into an
existing reaction kinetic framework. The delay term captures the
intrinsic lag associated with biodiesel formation during the
transesterification process. Furthermore, an optimal control
mechanism representing ultrasound-assisted production is
introduced to regulate biodiesel yield while minimizing control
effort. The resulting delayed optimal control system is analyzed
using Pontryagin’s Maximum Principle. The main objectives of
this work are therefore summarized as follows: (i) to develop a
delay-induced mathematical model describing biodiesel production dynamics; (ii) to incorporate an optimal control
mechanism representing ultrasound-assisted production; (iii)
to construct an efficient numerical scheme for solving the
resulting delay differential system; and (iv) to investigate the
influence of delay and key kinetic parameters on biodiesel
production dynamics through extensive numerical simulations
and sensitivity analysis.
The remainder of the paper is organized as follows: Section
“Formulation of the Delay-Induced Mathematical Model” and
Section “The Optimal Control Problem” presents the
formulation of the delay-induced biodiesel production system
together with the corresponding analysis of optimal controlinduced framework. Numerical experiments of the proposed
systems, sensitivity analysis, uncertainty analysis, and solver
validation results are discussed in Section “Results” while the

FORMULATION OF THE DELAY-INDUCED
MATHEMATICAL MODEL
The delayed biodiesel production model considered in this
study is developed by extending the kinetic transesterification
framework reported in ref 11 In the original formulation,
biodiesel synthesis from Jatropha curcas oil and methanol was
described through three reversible transesterification steps,
together with side saponification reactions in the presence of a
KOH catalyst. The model accounts for the conversion of
triglyceride, diglyceride, and monoglyceride into biodiesel and
glycerol, while also incorporating the influence of catalystmediated soap formation. In addition, the mass transfer
contribution induced by ultrasound irradiation was included
through the mass transfer coefficient, which depends on the
Reynolds and Schmidt numbers and reflects the enhancement of
molecular transport within the reaction vessel.
In practical biodiesel production, the formation of biodiesel is
not always an instantaneous process. The overall conversion
depends on intermediate reaction stages, catalyst activation,
molecular diffusion, alcohol−oil phase interaction, and ultrasound-assisted mass transfer.12−14 In ultrasound-assisted transesterification systems, transport enhancement and cavitationinduced mixing further influence the temporal evolution of the
reaction process.15,16 These mechanisms may introduce a finite
response time between the availability of reactants and the
measurable accumulation of biodiesel. To capture this memory
effect, we introduce a discrete time delay τ into the biodiesel
production term of the previously established kinetic model.
Specifically, the logistic production contribution is replaced by

DISCUSSION

In this study, a delay-induced mathematical model for biodiesel
production was investigated by incorporating a discrete time
delay into the production mechanism of an existing kinetic
model. The delay term represents the inherent reaction lag
associated with intermediate biochemical processes and catalytic
interactions during biodiesel synthesis. Furthermore, an optimal
control strategy was introduced to regulate the ultrasoundassisted production mechanism, allowing the system to balance
biodiesel yield against operational control costs.
The numerical investigations reveal several important
dynamical properties of the proposed delayed system. The
trajectory analysis demonstrates that increasing delay values
significantly influence the transient behavior of the system,
producing oscillatory dynamics before eventual stabilization.
Phase portrait analysis further confirms the existence of
structured dynamic patterns in the delayed system, which
demonstrates that delay can induce oscillatory responses in
biodiesel concentration and associated state variables.
To efficiently simulate the delayed nonlinear dynamics, a
fixed-step RK4-based solver for delay differential equations was
developed in this study. Numerical validation shows strong
agreement between the proposed solver and the widely used
ddeint method. The absolute error between the two
approaches remains small throughout the simulation interval,
confirming the accuracy of the proposed scheme as presented in
Algorithm 1. In addition, runtime comparisons demonstrate that
the proposed solver achieves comparable or improved computational efficiency, which highlights its suitability for large-scale
simulations and parametric investigations.
Sensitivity analysis provides further insight into the regulatory
mechanisms governing the oscillatory patterns. The PRCC
results indicate that certain reaction parameters exert strong
positive influence on oscillation amplitude, while others
contribute negatively. In particular, the reaction rate parameter
ρ5 exhibits the strongest positive contribution, whereas the delay
parameter τ plays a structural role by sustaining oscillatory
behavior through memory effects. The Spearman sensitivity
ranking confirms that a small subset of kinetic parameters
dominates the dynamical behavior of the system. Furthermore,
we have performed a global uncertainty analysis using violin
plots, which indicates that the oscillatory regime remains stable
under significant parameter variation. The absence of multimodal amplitude distributions further suggests that the observed
oscillatory responses remain consistent across the explored
parameter range, highlighting the robustness of the proposed
biodiesel production dynamics to moderate parametric
uncertainty.

effectiveness of the ultrasound-assisted control strategy in
enhancing biodiesel production under delayed reaction

CONCLUSIONS
Overall, this present study establishes a unified mathematical
framework that combines delay dynamics, ultrasound-assisted
optimal control, and efficient numerical simulation for biodiesel
production. Our investigations confirm that delayed reaction
effects together with optimized ultrasound regulation substantially alter the production dynamics and stability behavior of the
system. The developed delayed control framework provides a
systematic approach for improving biodiesel synthesis efficiency
and should serve as a useful as well as realistic theoretical
foundation for the design of more effective and economically
sustainable renewable fuel production strategies. Future studies
may undertake rigorous analytical investigations, including
delay-dependent local stability and Hopf bifurcation analyses,
to further characterize the dynamical properties of the proposed
delayed biodiesel production model.
