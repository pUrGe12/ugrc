(including ne slag, coarse slag, y ash, and bottom slag, etc.).1–4
Among these, coarse slag, primarily composed of amorphous
silicates and residual carbon, holds potential resource value,
and faces challenges in eﬃcient utilization due to poor understanding of its structure–activity relationship.5,6 Nevertheless,
the current methods of handling coarse slag are landlling and
stockpiling, resulting in low comprehensive utilization rate and

utilization. While these studies demonstrate the potential of
coarse gasication slag for various applications, systematic
comparative studies linking gasier type to coarse slag
structure-application relationships are lacking.
For the resource utilization of slag, it is important to reveal
its inherent properties (rst-principle study). Therefore, it is
essential to conduct a detailed analysis for the composition and
characteristics of the coarse slag to determine its optimal
utilization pathway. Additionally, kinetic analysis plays a crucial
role in understanding the reaction mechanisms of slag thermal
cracking and combustion, providing theoretical support for
practical applications.21,22 However, existing studies have only
qualitatively analyzed the thermal behavior of gasier slag,
leaving key quantitative kinetic parameters, such as activation
energy distributions, pre-exponential factors, reaction rate
constants, and the optimal reaction order model-unavailable
for coarse slags from specic gasiers. This knowledge gap
signicantly restricts the precise regulation and rened
resource utilization of gasier slag in heat treatment and
modication processes.
In this study, coarse slag samples based on Zhundong coal
were collected from three diﬀerent gasiers: Texaco gasier (CSSH), opposed four-nozzle gasier (CS-YK), and Jinhua gasier
(CS-TY). A systematic comparison of the composition, structure
characteristics, and thermogravimetric behavior of the three
slag samples was conducted using methods such as crystal
structure analysis, microscopic morphology observation, and
functional group analysis. Furthermore, the Coats-Redfern
method was adopted to quantitatively determine the kinetic

2.

Experimental section

2.1

Raw materials

Coarse slag samples were collected from three coal gasication
processes with Zhundong coal (Xinjiang) as gasication raw
material, and labeled as CS-SH (Texaco gasier), CS-YK
(opposed four-nozzle gasier) and CS-TY (Jinhua gasier),
respectively. The three samples were placed in a fume hood to
dry at room temperature, and ground for use with a FW-100
crusher for 40 seconds.
2.2

Gasication source

Fig. S1 shows the process source of three types of coarse slag.
Fig. S1(a) employs a single-nozzle top-mounted spray design.
Coal-water slurry and oxygen undergo partial oxidation at 1300–
1400 °C and 4.0–6.5 MPa, with a residence time of 2–5 s. The
product is rapidly quenched by a water bath in the quenching
chamber to obtain coarse slag (CS-SH). Fig. S1(b) utilizes
opposed four nozzles to enhance turbulent mixing. It operates
at temperature of 1250–1350 °C, pressure of 3.8–4.2 MPa, and
residence time of 3–6 s. The product is slowly cooled through
a downcomer before entering the quench chamber water bath,
yielding coarse slag (CS-YK). Fig. S1(c) features a combined
design of a water-cooled wall gasication chamber and
a radiant waste heat boiler.23 The gasication occurs at 1350–
1450 °C under lower pressure (2.5–3.5 MPa). The cooled slag is
periodically discharged into a slag pool through the slag
removal system, producing coarse slag (CS-TY).
2.3

Analytical methods

2.3.1 Composition and structure characteristics analyses.
According to GB/T212-2008, a sample mass of 1 g was used for
proximate analysis to determine the moisture, ash and volatile
in the residue samples, and the xed carbon content was
calculated. Ultimate analysis was performed using an Elemantar Vario EL cube elemental analyzer (Elementar UNICUBE,
Germany) according to GB/T 476-2008.
The crystal structure of the samples was characterized by Xray diﬀractometer (XRD, Bruker D8 Advance, Germany). The
scanning 2q range was 10–80°, step size 0.02°, scanning rate 2°/
min. Ash composition of the sample was analyzed by X-ray
uorescence spectrometer (XRF, ZSX Primus III+, Japan), and
the test method and mode were tablet method and oxide
method, respectively. Functional group structure types were
analyzed using infrared spectroscopy (FTIR, Thermo Scientic
Nicolet iS20, USA). Spectral resolution was 4 cm−1, scanning
times 32, and the mass ratio of sample to KBr was 1 : 100. The
degree of graphitization of the carbon was characterized by

The laser wavelength was 532 nm with xed laser power.

2.3.2 Thermogravimetric analyses. Thermogravimetric
analyzer (TG, HITACHISTA200, Japan) was used to test the
thermal cracking and combustion characteristics of the
samples from room temperature to 1000 °C with high purity
nitrogen and air as carrier gas, and the heating rate was set as
20 °C min−1. The test sample mass was 8–10 mg, alumina
crucible was applied, and gas ow rate was maintained at 50
mL min−1. For the re-gasication performance, the sample was
heated to 900 °C with the rate of 20 °C min−1, standing for
10 min, at the condition of nitrogen. Subsequently, the atmosphere was switched to CO2, at the temperature of 900 °C, for
30 min of reaction period, and the gasication reactivity can be
evaluated.
This research employed the standard Coats-Redfern kinetic
equation under a single heating rate to calculate the kinetic
parameters of ash thermal cracking/combustion.24 Specic
temperature interval was designated for tting each reaction
order n. To compare the combustion performance of the
samples, the comprehensive combustion index S is calculated.
And the carbon conversion X and the gasication reaction rate R
are used to characterize the gasication characteristics of CSSH, CS-YK, and CS-TY samples.25

Results and discussion

3.1 Composition and structure characteristics of CS-SH, CSYK, and CS-TY
3.1.1 Proximate and ultimate analyses. Proximate and
ultimate analyses of CS-SH, CS-YK, and CS-TY are summarized
in Table 1. Three coarse slags exhibit high ash content and low
volatiles, indicating that most organic components in the coal
were converted into gaseous fuels during gasication. The
residues primarily consist of unreacted carbonaceous particles
and inorganic minerals. CS-SH shows a signicantly higher
xed carbon content (42.31%) compared to CS-YK (0.88%) and
CS-TY (0.26%), indicating that it has a high content of carbonaceous components and is suitable as carbon based raw
material or co-gasication feed with raw coal to achieve high
value-added utilization.26 CS-YK and CS-TY possess ash
contents of 98.47% and 93.26%, respectively, with surface
carbon contents as high as 86.81% and 81.04% (Fig. 4), suggesting that the two samples show a relatively high proportion
of inorganic minerals, which is suitable for use as building
materials or further utilization through carbon-ash separation.27 Compared with CS-SH (0.09) and CS-YK (0.48), CS-TY

3.1.2 Ash composition. Table S1 presents the ash composition of CS-SH, CS-YK, and CS-TY. Combined with Table 1, the
ash contents of CS-SH, CS-YK, and CS-TY were 52.89%, 98.47%,
and 93.26%, respectively, primarily composed of inorganic
components such as SiO2, Al2O3, Fe2O3, and CaO. According to
ASTM C 618-19 and GB/T 212-2008 (Fly Ash for Cement and
Concrete), CS-SH and CS-YK exhibit SiO2 + Al2O3 + Fe2O3 ratios
approaching 70% with similar element distributions, and CaO
contents of 20.22% and 17.75%, meeting the criteria of
medium-calcium y ash (N-grade). CS-TY shows a SiO2 + Al2O3 +
Fe2O3 ratio of 85.90%, CaO content of 6.71%, and SO3 content
of 0.42%, complying with low-calcium y ash (F-grade) standards. These properties make its ash suitable as raw materials
for construction and concrete production, and reagent for soil
amendment by improving fertility and structure stability.28
Additionally, the ash component distribution of CS-SH and CSYK is similar to that of basic glass (CaO and MgO are glass
network modiers, and SiO2 and Al2O3 are glass network
forming agents), which is conducive to the formation of glass
system and the preparation of foam glass-ceramics.29 Notably,
CS-TY exhibits high SiO2 content (57.38%), with SiO2+Al2O3
accounting for 75.99%, resulting in a high Si/Al ratio. This
characteristic makes it a promising candidate for synthesizing
porous material such as molecular sieve for adsorption
applications.
3.1.3 Crystalline and amorphous phase structures. Fig. 1(a)
displays the XRD patterns of the three gasication coarse slags.
Overall, the patterns exhibit a broad hump in 12–60° region,
with few sharp and intense diﬀraction peaks. This indicates
that the mineral phases in the coal underwent complete melting
under high-temperature condition, followed by decomposition
and mutual reactions. And the molten slag, rapidly quenched,
formed residues dominated by amorphous glassy matrix.30 Two
small and sharp quartz (SiO2) diﬀraction peaks were observed at
26.64° and 27.80°. Additionally, a broad and weak peak between
20-30° suggests the presence of amorphous components in all
samples.

particles and ne black powdery particles can be observed for

presents barely translucent substances. In Fig. 1(c), CS-YK
sample shows irregular yellow aky particles. Meanwhile,
Fig. 1(d) shows that the sample of CS-TY comprises irregular
yellow aky particles and colorless granules. The irregular black

carbon-encapsulated amorphous aluminosilicates, forming

and Fe, with trace carbon content.32–34 These correspond to

3.1.8 Specic surface area and aperture. The adsorption–
desorption isotherms and pore size distribution curves are
shown in Fig. 4(b and c), with analytical results summarized in
Table S5. CS-SH exhibits a hybrid Type I + Type IV isotherm,
with a BET surface area of 153.23 m2 g−1, signicantly higher
than those of CS-YK (13.94 m2 g−1) and CS-TY (34.26 m2 g−1). Its
micropore surface area (139.36 m2 g−1) accounts for 90.95% of
the total surface area, while the micropore volume 73.55% of
total pore volume dominates. Additionally, CS-SH has the
largest total pore volume (0.13 cm3 g−1) among the three slags,
indicating a highly developed porous structure. These properties suggest its suitability as a high-eﬃciency adsorption
material. In addition, the carbon-coated ash structure, the
particle accumulation state of gasication residue and the
inherent mineral skeleton structure limit the pore expansion,
resulting in lower specic surface area than the synthetic
mesoporous materials with controllable pore structure.12 CS-YK
predominantly follows a Type I isotherm with no distinct
hysteresis loop. CS-TY displays a Type I isotherm with an H3type hysteresis loop. Despite its low total pore volume (0.03
cm3 g−1), it exhibits a high micropore density (Smic = 22.74 m2
g−1). The narrow pore channels and potential “ink-bottle” pore
structures hinder gas desorption, while the lack of mesopores
and open micropores limits its adsorption capacity for large
molecules. However, its high Si/Al ratio (SiO2/Al2O3 z 2.5) and
uniform micropores suggest potential for synthesizing zeolite
molecular sieves via hydrothermal methods.37

medium calcium y ash. The main reason for this diﬀerence is
the diﬀerence of raw material sources and ash composition. The
gasication residue formed stable and dense mineral crystal
phase and dense particle structure through violent hightemperature reduction atmosphere reaction. At the same
time, the special surface carbon enrichment increases the
reaction barrier. Compared with the conventional medium
calcium y ash formed under oxidation combustion conditions,
the chemical bond breaking and initiating reaction require
higher energy, so as to improve the activation energy.
3.2.3 Gasication characteristics. Fig. S4 illustrates the
relationship between carbon conversion and gasication time
(a) and gasication rate versus carbon conversion (b) for the
three slags during CO2 gasication. The gasication reaction
primarily involves two processes: surface gasication of particles and shrinking-core model where reaction progresses from
the exterior to the interior of particles.41 From Fig. S4(a), the
trend of carbon conversion increasing with time is similar for
all the three slags. However, CS-TY achieves complete gasication in a shorter time compared to CS-SH and CS-YK, due to
aluminosilicate minerals encapsulating carbon particles, which
hinders the contact between carbon particles and CO2.
Fig. S4(b) reveals that CS-YK exhibits higher reaction rate in the
early stage of gasication. As carbon conversion increases, the
surface carbon is consumed by CO2, and the particle size and

surface area remain relatively constant. The reaction transitions
to a shrinking-core model, during which CS-TY gradually
surpasses CS-YK and CS-SH in reaction rate. However,
compared to raw coal or other coal gasication solid wastes

signicant advantages as re-gasication feedstocks.

4. Conclusions
Three crude slags (CS-SH, CS-YK, CS-TY) exhibit diﬀerences in
composition and structure characteristics, leading to distinct
performance in thermal cracking, combustion, and adsorption.
This study lls the existing research gap and veries that
gasier type exerts systematic regulation on slag microscopic
structure, further determining the optimal resource utilization
pathway of each residue.
CS-SH features higher xed carbon content (42.31%) and
specic surface area (153.23 m2 g−1), exhibiting a “carboncoated ash” structure, presenting lower rst-order reaction
activation energy (81.41 kJ mol−1) and higher comprehensive
combustion index (1.6 × 10−7%2 min−2$°C−3), which makes it
suitable for applications in gas adsorption, wastewater treatment and co-combustion fuel elds. CS-YK shows higher ash
content of 98.47%, primarily composed of inorganic minerals
such as SiO2, Al2O3, and Fe2O3. Additionally, this residue
demonstrates remarkable activation energies of 44.18 kJ mol−1
(n = 3) and 110.21 kJ mol−1 (n = 1) for thermal cracking and
combustion, and lower comprehensive combustion index (2.33
× 10−10%2 min−2$°C−3), making it as a high-quality building
material for cement admixtures or ceramic production.
The ash content of CS-TY is as high as 93.26%, mainly
composed of SiO2 and Al2O3. The carbon particles in this
sample are wrapped by dense mineral layer, forming an “ashcoated carbon” structure, restricting adsorption and combustion performance. In addition, it can be used as raw material for
heat-resistant building materials. Furthermore, CS-TY has
a SiO2/Al2O3 ratio of 2.5, which makes it a candidate for
preparing high-performance porous material such as molecular
sieve.
