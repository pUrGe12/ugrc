eﬄuent, corn straw was used as a substrate for dark fermentation to produce hydrogen, and diﬀerent
substrates (butyric acid, acetic acid, and fermentation eﬄuent) were used to enrich the bioanode of the
microbial electrolysis cell (MEC). The eﬀects of the anode enrichment method, substrate concentration
and applied voltage on hydrogen production from butyrate-type straw fermentation eﬄuent were
investigated. The microbial community structure was analysed by high-throughput sequencing. The
results showed that the maximum hydrogen yield and hydrogen production rate reached 943 mL g−1
and 3.62 m3 m−3 d−1, respectively, when the bioanode enriched with butyric acid was used to treat the
straw fermentation eﬄuent at 0.6 V applied voltage. Compared with the enrichment of fermentation
eﬄuent, the degradation rate of butyric acid and the removal rate of chemical oxygen demand (COD)
increased by 35.1% and 25%, respectively. The anode enriched with butyric acid had higher species
richness and diversity, and the abundance of butyric acid oxidizing bacteria Syntrophomonas was as high

Introduction

Crop straw is an important cellulose biomass resource. As
a traditional agricultural country, China is rich in crop straw
resources.1,2 However, except for a small amount of straw used
as livestock feed or organic fertilizer, most of it is abandoned or
burned, which not only pollutes the environment but also
wastes biological resources. Therefore, it is of great signicance
to use straw waste for clean energy production and waste
resource recycling. The technology for producing biohydrogen
through dark fermentation has emerged as a research hotspot
in hydrogen generation, primarily because of its gentle reaction
conditions, easy operation, and diverse range of substrates.3,4
Butyrate-type fermentation is the main type of hydrogen
production by dark fermentation from straw biomass.5 The
liquid metabolic products resulting from butyrate-type
fermentation primarily consist of volatile fatty acids and

alcohols, including butyric acid, acetic acid, propionic acid, and
ethanol.6,7 The discharge of these by-products not only causes
secondary environmental pollution but also leads to the waste
of renewable resources. Therefore, the further conversion of
organic matter in the fermentation by-products into clean
hydrogen energy is the key to improving the utilization eﬃciency of straw biomass resources.
As an emerging electrochemical-assisted biohydrogen
production technology, microbial electrolysis cell (MEC) can
utilize a variety of eﬄuent to produce hydrogen, which has the
advantages of high hydrogen yield and energy eﬃciency.8,9
Many researchers have coupled MEC with the fermentation
process for cascade hydrogen production, which eﬀectively
improves the utilization eﬃciency of resources.10 Although MEC
can eﬀectively convert acetic acid and ethanol in eﬄuent to
produce hydrogen, the degradation rate of long-chain organics
such as butyric acid and propionic acid is low. Liu et al. used
hydrogen production eﬄuent as a substrate, the removal rate of
butyric acid by MEC was less than 20%, while the removal rate
of acetic acid was more than 90%.11 Li et al. used a singlechamber with dual-anode MEC to treat straw fermentation
eﬄuent to produce hydrogen.12 The butyric acid removal rate
was merely 4%. Due to this limited removal eﬃciency of butyric

methods on microbial community structure were studied by

2 Experimental
2.1

Experimental materials

Corn straw was taken from the Science and Technology Park of
Henan Agricultural University. Before use, the corn straw was
naturally dried, crushed, and passed through a 40-mesh sieve
for use. The hydrogen-producing strain Clostridium sartagoforme FZ11 preserved in the laboratory was used for dark
fermentation of straw. The inoculation source of MEC was cow
dung compost (taken from Zhengzhou dairy farm, China). The
compost was diluted with distilled water at a solid–liquid ratio
of 1 : 10, and microwave radiation was used to pretreat the
compost for 1.5 min to inhibit methanogen activity.13 The cow
dung compost that had been pretreated underwent centrifugation, aer which the precipitate from the lower layer was gathered as a bacterial source and kept in a refrigerator at 4 °C for
later use.
2.2

MECs construction

The experiment used a single-chamber double-anode MEC
reactor (Fig. 1). The reactor had a cubic conguration with
dimensions of 40 × 40 × 40 mm and a total volume of 64 mL.
The MEC anode consisted of two circular carbon felts with
a diameter of 30 mm and a thickness of 5 mm. The cathode
consisted of a 30 mm × 30 mm carbon cloth. The carbon cloth

2.3

Dark fermentation hydrogen production

The hydrogen-producing strain C. sartagoforme FZ11 was precultured on seed medium, and then the early logarithmic phase
culture was transferred to the straw dark fermentation medium
with the inoculation amount of 10% (V/V), and the fermentation
bottle was placed in a constant temperature incubator. The
fermentation temperature was (35 ± 1) °C and the rotation
speed was 120 rpm for the fermentation hydrogen production
test. The concentration of hydrogen in the gas phase product
and the concentration of various acids and alcohols in the
liquid phase were determined at a certain time interval. All tests
were performed in triplicate. The composition of the seed
medium of C. sartagoforme FZ11 was as follows: 10 g L−1
glucose, 4 g L−1 tryptone, 1 g L−1 K2HPO4, 1 g L−1 KH2PO4, 3 g
L−1 NaCl, 0.1 g L−1 MgCl2, 0.5 g L−1 L-cysteine, 0.1 g L−1
FeSO4$7H2O, pH 7.0. The composition of the fermentation
medium was as follows: 10 g L−1 raw corn straw, 3 g L−1
peptone, 1 g L−1 yeast extract, 0.5 g L−1 L-cysteine, and 10 mL
nutrient solution. The above substances were dissolved in 1 L of
KH2PO4-Na2HPO4 buﬀer (pH 6.47, 0.1 mol L−1). The composition of the nutrient solution: 0.00278 g L−1 FeCl2, 0.01 g L−1
NaCl, 0.1 g L−1 MgSO4$7H2O, 0.01 g L−1 CaCl2$2H2O, 0.01 g L−1
Na2MoO4$2H2O, 0.015 g L−1 MnSO4$7H2O.
2.4

fuel cell (MFC) mode. The MFC reactor adopts a single-chamber
air cathode with oxygen as the cathode electron acceptor.

into the MFC anodic enrichment medium (12.5 mL L−1 vitamin
solution and 12.5 mL L−1 mineral solution and corresponding
substrates were added to 50 mmolL−1 phosphate buﬀer). The
substrate was 2 g L−1 butyric acid, 2 g L−1 sodium acetate, or 2fold diluted fermentation eﬄuent, and the pH of the medium
was adjusted to 7.0. When the MFC reaches the maximum
stable output voltage for ve consecutive cycles, the anode
enrichment is completed. The enriched bioanode was transferred to the MEC reactor as an anode. The composition of the
MEC hydrogen production medium includes 12.5 mL L−1
vitamin solution, and 12.5 mL L−1 mineral solution and corresponding substrates were added to 100 mmol L−1 phosphate
buﬀer. The substrates were hydrogen fermentation eﬄuent of
diﬀerent dilutions. High-purity nitrogen (99.99%) was used to
purge for 10 min to remove oxygen in the MEC reactor. The
external voltage was provided by the battery detection system,
and the batch test was carried out at (36 ± 1)°C in a constant

2.5

Analysis method

The hydrogen content was determined by gas chromatograph
(Aglient-7890B) equipped with thermal conductivity detector
(TCD). The chromatographic column was a 6-foot Porapak Q
(80/100 mesh) stainless steel packed column, high purity
nitrogen was used as the carrier gas, and manual injection was
performed. The injection volume was 0.4 mL.14 Aglient-7820A
gas chromatograph equipped with a hydrogen ame detector
(FID) was used to determine the content of volatile fatty acid
and alcohols in the fermentation waste liquid. The specic
determination conditions were as follows: capillary column,
10% PEG-20 mesh+2% H3PO4 silanized 102 support (80/100
mesh), high purity nitrogen as carrier gas, manual injection,
injection volume 1 mL.15 COD content was determined by the
microwave digestion method.16 First, 5 mL sample was taken
with a pipette and placed in a digestion tank. A uniform
quantity of catalyst and digestion solution was incorporated
into the sample, which was then subjected to digestion in
a microwave oven. Following digestion and subsequent cooling
to ambient temperature, the liquid was moved to a 150 mL
conical ask. Next, 2–3 drops of test ferrous indicator were
introduced, and the standard solution was titrated using
ammonium ferrous sulfate standard solution. The color change
was observed, and the amount of standard solution at the end of
titration was recorded. The calculation of COD concentration is
shown in formula (1).

3.2.2 The current density of MEC. The current density of
MEC under diﬀerent bioanode enrichment methods is shown
in the Fig. 5. At a voltage of 0.6 V, the maximum current density
recorded for MEC enriched with sodium acetate (AF-MEC)
reached 295 A m−3, whereas the peak current density for MEC
enriched with fermentation eﬄuent (EF-MEC) was merely 197 A
m−3. When the butyric acid enrichment method (BF-MEC) was
used, the maximum current density was 375 A m−3, which is
much higher than the former two. The reason is that diﬀerent
enrichment methods will aﬀect the structure of anode electrogenic bacteria, resulting in a huge diﬀerence in current density.
With the decrease of substrate concentration, the current
density increases. When the MEC inuent was FE2 (2 times
dilution), the current density of MEC reached the maximum
under diﬀerent enrichment methods. As the substrate concentration decreased, the current density did not change much.

the current density of the reactor was reduced to 121 A m−3 (EFMEC), 210 A m−3 (AF-MEC), 277 A m−3 (BF-MEC). It shows that
the concentration of eﬄuent has an important eﬀect on the
hydrogen production of MEC. The higher the concentration, the
more complex the composition of eﬄuent and the more
obvious the metabolic inhibition of anode electrogenic
microorganisms.

can oxidize butyric acid and produce acetic acid and H2 under

is not an ideal substrate type for electrogenic bacteria. Butyric
acid might be rst degraded into acetic acid and then used by
electrogenic bacteria. Therefore, we infer that butyric acid is
rst degraded into acetic acid by butyric acid oxidizing bacteria
Syntrophomonas, and then acetic acid is used by electrogenic
bacteria. This can better explain the phenomenon of higher
current density and increased acetic acid content in the
hydrogen production process of BF-MEC. The experimental
results show that the butyric acid in fermentation eﬄuent needs
to be rst converted into acetic acid that can be used by electrogenic bacteria, and then it can be further used by MEC to
produce hydrogen. Therefore, the conversion of butyric acid is
the rate-controlling step of MEC hydrogen production. In the
EF-MEC anode community, the dominant species were widely
distributed, such as Geobacter (15.4%), Dysgonomonas (11.1%),
and Clostridium (11.5%). Although there are also butyric acidoxidizing bacteria, Syntrophomonas (5.2%) and Pseudomonas
(1.1%), the abundance of its electricity-producing bacteria,
Geobacter, is only 15.4%, which is not conducive to the
conversion of electrons and the production of hydrogen. The
reason may be that the composition of fermentation eﬄuent is
complex, which is not conducive to the growth and enrichment
of electrogenic bacteria. At the same time, the unclassied
bacteria at the genus level of the three communities accounted
for 24.7% (AF-MEC), 35.2% (BF-MEC), and 31.3% (EF-MEC) of
the total sequence, respectively. This shows that the bacteria in
the anode community domesticated with butyric acid and straw
fermentation eﬄuent are more complex.

In this study, corn straw was used as a substrate for dark
fermentation to produce hydrogen, and the fermentation
eﬄuent was used as the feed of the MEC, and the butyric acid,
acetic acid, and fermentation eﬄuent were used to enrich the
MEC bioanode. The results showed that the maximum hydrogen
yield of 943 mL g−1 and hydrogen production rate of 3.62 m3 m−3
d−1 were obtained by using butyric acid enriched bioanode MEC
(BF-MEC) with fermentation eﬄuent at 0.6 V applied voltage.
Compared with the bioanode enriched by fermentation eﬄuent,
the butyric acid degradation rate and COD removal rate in
eﬄuent increased by 35.1% and 25%, respectively. Illumina
MiSeq high-throughput sequencing technology was used to
investigate the eﬀects of diﬀerent bioanode enrichment methods
on the community structure of MEC anode biolm. The results
showed that the bioanode enriched with butyric acid had higher
species richness and community diversity than the bioanode
enriched with acetic acid and fermentation eﬄuent. It is worth
noting that the abundance of butyric acid oxidizing bacteria
Syntrophomonas in the MEC anode community enriched with
butyric acid is as high as 8.7%. Therefore, we infer that butyric
acid is rst degraded by butyric acid-oxidizing bacteria Syntrophomonas into acetic acid, and then acetic acid is used by electrogenic bacteria, in which the oxidation of butyric acid is the
rate-controlling step aﬀecting MEC hydrogen production. Aer
two-stage cascade hydrogen production from straw waste, the
fermentation eﬄuent was puried synchronously. The ndings
of this research will provide a useful reference for large-scale
biological hydrogen generation from straw.
